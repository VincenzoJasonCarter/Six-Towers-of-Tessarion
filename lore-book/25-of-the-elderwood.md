# Of The Elderwood

*Excerpt from **The Crownweave Compendium**, Fourth Roadwarden’s Edition*  
 *Shelfmark: TV-EW-001, “On the Wood Beyond the Sound”*

---

### **Of the Elderwood, and of Sip**

Every chart of the Western Reach that the Compendium holds stops in the same place. West of the Sound, past the fishing villages and the white mountains the fjord-folk call **the Antlers**, the cartographer’s ink thins into a wash of green with no names on it, and then the paper ends. The Compendium has never been able to discover whether anybody drew further. It has, more than once, been able to discover that somebody tried.

The green is a forest. The fjord-folk call it **the Elderwood**, and the elves of the ring call it *Kehvireth*, the deer’s grove, and neither name is a district’s, because the Elderwood is not Thal’vireth’s. It is the forest that stood in the west before the Verdant Tower was planted with anyone’s grief, the part of it the tree never took, and it has a keeper of its own: a white stag older than the Count, whom the elves call **Sip** and the hunters of the Sound call, more carefully, **the Old Stag**.

Thal’vireth is the grove that remembers. **The Elderwood is the one that does not.** It does not keep the Count, it does not keep the Ledger, and, those who go into it will tell you if they can, it does not keep very much of anything else.

---

### **I. The Temper of the Wood**

The Elderwood is a place of **true hours and short memories**.

Its time is ordinary. A day under its trees is a day at sea, and a traveller who goes in at the spring tide comes out at a later one, no older and no younger than the calendar says. The Count does not reach it: no district’s pace runs under its canopy, no Count-gate stands at its edge, and the drums of Balamir cannot be heard there however still the air. To an elf of the ring this is strange enough. What is stranger is what the wood does instead.

**The Elderwood forgets.** Footprints fill in behind those who make them, sooner than the ground explains. A path walked in the morning is not there in the afternoon. A cairn built to mark a turning is found, a day later, as a scatter of stones with moss already on them. Travellers lose small things first: the name of the stream they crossed, which pocket the flint is in, the face of the friend who is waiting at the edge. Those who stay longer lose more. The fjord-folk have a word for the look of someone who has been in the wood too long, and the Compendium has been unable to get any of them to remember it.

Ink fades there too, and this is the part the Curia minds. A **Ledger of Hours** carried into the Elderwood greys from its last stamp backwards, a page or so a week, until the hand that wrote it cannot be read. **Count-beads** lose their colour in the same way, green and amber and black alike going to the dull grey of river stone. The elves call this *the Greying*. It stops when the Ledger leaves the wood. It does not undo itself.

Nor does it stay in the wood. The same entries grey, page for page and in the same week, in the **great register** at Tun’iel, three hundred kilometres away in the slowest ground in the realm, where nothing else has faded in living memory. The archivists of the Heartwood Stacks have watched it happen to a single line, under glass, with a Daykeeper on either side of the desk. They have no explanation for it that they are willing to put in writing, and they have stopped trying to recopy the lines, because the copies grey too.

The Daykeepers explain all of this by the tree. Ix’kaanil feeds on memory, they say, and spends it, and the Count is what that spending looks like; the Elderwood is simply ground where the tree has never been allowed to eat, and so there is no memory there to spend, only the kind that drains away into the soil as it does anywhere, a great deal faster. The fjord-folk explain it by the stag, and say so more briefly: *the Old Stag keeps the wood, and he does not keep what isn’t his.*

---

### **II. Those Who Walk It**

Very few people go into the Elderwood, and fewer of them on purpose.

The **hunters of the Sound** are the wood’s only regular visitors: fjord-folk from Kaynab, Saknab and the villages under the Antlers, who have hunted its edge for as long as there have been villages to hunt for. They go in for deer, boar and the long-furred black squirrel of the western pines, they go in by the old rite (see *Law at the Edge*, below), and they never go further than a day’s walk from the treeline, by long custom and longer experience. A hunter of the Sound who goes deeper is said to have *gone to ask the stag himself*, which is the polite way of saying that they have not come back.

The **Balam of Kehir** keep the realm’s one post at the wood’s edge. It is a quiet posting and a strange one: the Order sends its steadiest warriors there, and rotates them out after a single outside year, because those who serve longer begin to mislay things.

The **unkept** go in to be forgotten. Some are debtors, some convicts, some heirs, lovers or Ah K’in’s clerks with reasons of their own; what they have in common is a Ledger they would rather nobody could read. The Balam try to stop them at the edge and bring them back if they cannot. The Compendium has heard, from three unrelated sources on the Sound, that there is a camp somewhere in the deep wood where the unkept who stayed live together without names, and that they are content. It has not been able to confirm this, and notes that none of its sources could say how they knew.

The **dying** come, now and then, because of a story: that the Old Stag heals what is brought to him wounded, and that this is true of people as well as deer, at a price (see *The Mending Hollow*, below). Most are turned back at Kehir. Some are carried past it at night.

And there are the **surveyors**. For some years now, small parties of travellers in grey coats have come down through the Hushwood from the north, saying they are surveyors from Crownweave, and asked in the villages of the Sound the way into the Elderwood. They pay well and in Crownsblood. They carry instruments no Crownweave surveyor carries. The Balam at Siisir and Witzir believe they are Sangrith, and that they are looking for something in particular. The fjord-folk believe that they find it, because none of them has ever asked the way out.

---

### **III. Of Sip, the Old Stag**

The guardian of the Elderwood is a **white stag** of great size and greater age, standing half again the height of a horse at the shoulder and moving among the trees with no sound at all.

Those who have seen him agree on most things. His coat is white, not grey, and clean in any weather. His antlers branch as wide as a cottage roof, are grown over with moss and with small white flowers that bloom out of season, and are broken, here and there, the way an old tree’s crown is broken. Between them he carries a **nest of wild wasps** the size of a barrel, which hums when he is near and is the first thing any hunter of the Sound listens for. Deer follow him through the wood at a respectful distance, as do wounded animals of every other kind. No one who has seen him can afterwards say what colour his eyes are, though all of them looked.

He does not speak, or has never spoken to anyone the Compendium can find. He is not cruel. He is, by every account, attentive: to the hunter who asks leave and to the one who does not, to the animal taken cleanly and to the one left to bleed, to the bones returned and to the bones kept. Hunters who keep the rite say they have seen him watching from the treeline, and been let go. Hunters who break it say nothing, because they are not found, or are found at the forest’s edge some days later, unhurt, with no memory of where they have been and nothing in their hands.

The elves call him **Sip**, a name from the old tongue that the Daykeepers say means only the deer’s lord, and is older than any of the realm’s other words. They do not pray to him. They do not plant him in their calendars or carve him on their gates. But the oldest root-beds of the ring have, at their western edges, a small stone with antlers cut into it that nobody will explain.

**The Bargain of the Bones.** The fjord-folk tell, and the Curia does not deny, that when the First Keeper buried her shard among the Tower’s roots and the tree began to grow, the Old Stag came down out of the west to the place where Kehir now stands and waited there, and that she went out to meet him. What they said is not recorded. What they agreed is kept by everyone on the Sound: **the dead of the people go to the tree, and the dead of the forest go to the stag.** This, the hunters say, is why the elves of the ring never plant an animal, why the Count stops at the edge of the Elderwood, and why the tree, which eats every other memory in the west, has never eaten this one.

There is a second story, which the Curia does deny. The First Keeper is remembered only as the First Keeper because nobody knows her name. On the Sound they say that this is because she went back to the Old Stag at the end of her life, alone, to ask him something, and that her name is in the Elderwood still.

### **IV. Law at the Edge**

The Elderwood lies inside Thal’vireth’s claim and outside its Count, and the law has had to decide what that means.

At **Kehir**, the Balam post on the wood’s eastern edge, a Daykeeper keeps the **Grey Ledger**: a register of everyone who goes into the Elderwood by the post, with the hour they went in, and the hour, if any, at which they came out. On the way in, the Daykeeper stamps a traveller’s own Ledger with the grey seal of Kehir; on the way out, with a second. The pages between the two seals are greyed by then and cannot be read, and the law reads them as what they are. **Time spent in the Elderwood is entered as no time at all.** A traveller who spends a month under the trees comes back a month older by the sea-calendar and not one day older by the Ledger, which is to say, at law, not at all.

This would matter less if it stopped there. It does not. A Ledger kept long enough in the wood greys entirely, from its last stamp back to its first, and so does the great register’s copy of it, birth-entry and all. A citizen whose Ledger cannot be read in either place is a citizen whose age, debts, sentence, inheritance and day-name cannot be proved, by anyone, ever again. To go into the Elderwood meaning to stay there until this happens is called **going unkept**, and it is the only crime in Thal’vireth that the realm has to catch its criminal *before* they commit it. The Balam of Kehir are charged with stopping the unkept at the edge and bringing them back if they get past it. They are not required to go further than a day’s walk to do so, and do not.

**The hunters’ rite** is older than the realm’s law and the realm has the sense to leave it alone. A hunter going into the Elderwood:

* asks leave at the **Asking Stone** at the treeline, aloud, saying what they mean to take;
* takes only that, and kills cleanly, and follows a wounded animal until it is dead or lost;
* brings the meat home and the bones back, and lays them on the **Bone-Cairns** beside the Stone within the month.

The Balam keep the rite as carefully as the fjord-folk do, and expect foreigners to. A Crownweave sportsman who shot a deer in the Elderwood for its antlers some years ago was put on the next ship out of Nabireth by the Curia, at the request of the Order, and is understood to have been grateful to go.

There is, finally, the question of the stag himself. The Compact says nothing of him. The Curia says nothing of him. The Balam Order, which is sworn to the Tree, has a standing instruction regarding him which its war-captain has read and its warriors have not, and which is said to be one line long.

### **V. Certain Places of Note**

#### **1\. The Asking Stone**

*Where leave is asked.*

At the treeline beside Kehir, where the hunters’ path goes into the wood, stands a tall grey stone, rough and uncut, with a hollow worn in its eastern face at about the height of a kneeling hunter’s hand. This is the **Asking Stone**, where those who go into the Elderwood ask leave to: hunters for their game, foraging parties for their roots and resin, and, on rare occasions, the Balam for whoever they have come to fetch.

The asking is spoken aloud and kept short. There is no answer that anyone has ever heard. Hunters say that a hunt which has been granted feels, at the treeline, like stepping into cool water, and one that has not feels like nothing at all, and that it is the second that should frighten you. The hollow in the stone is always full of small offerings: a pinch of salt, a twist of maize-husk, a bead. A Count-bead left there greys overnight.

---

#### **2\. The Bone-Cairns**

*Where the forest’s dead are given back.*

Behind the Asking Stone, along the treeline north and south, run the **Bone-Cairns**: low heaps of antler, skull and long-bone, laid down by the hunters of the Sound to return what they have taken. The oldest are mounded over with moss and mostly earth. The newest are white.

The cairns are not kept or tidied. Things take bones from them: foxes, the black squirrels, rain. Hunters say that a cairn is never found disturbed the morning after a stag-moon, and that they have never seen anything come for it. It is the one place in the wood that the Balam of Kehir will not walk at night, and the only place where they say they feel watched.

---

#### **3\. The Grey Mile**

*Where the forgetting begins.*

There is no line drawn on the ground where the Elderwood starts to forget. But about a mile in from the Asking Stone, by the reckoning of every hunter who has paced it, Ledger-stamps begin to fade, footprints begin to fill, and travellers begin, very gently, to lose the thread of what they were saying. The hunters call this stretch **the Grey Mile**, and the rite of the Sound is to turn back before the end of it unless the stag has given leave to go further.

Within the Grey Mile, it is said, a traveller loses only what they do not hold on to. Past it, they lose what they do. The Balam of Kehir tie a cord to their wrist at the treeline and knot it once for every hour they walk, so that they will know, if they come to themselves in the wood, how long they have been gone.

---

#### **4\. The Antlers**

*The white mountains in the west.*

From Ek’thalas, on a clear day, a traveller can look north-west over the forest to a range of glaciated mountains rising straight out of it: two broad massifs of white ice, branching into lesser peaks, which the fjord-folk call **the Antlers** and the Balam call nothing at all. The Elderwood wraps around their feet on every side but the east.

No one is known to have climbed them. The hunters of the Sound say that the Old Stag goes up into them in the depth of winter, when the wasps sleep, and comes down in the spring to shed. The Compendium’s own correspondent at Ek’thalas has watched them for eleven years and reports that the glaciers on their upper slopes stand at exactly the same line on the same day of every year, to within a pace, which no glacier the Compendium knows of has ever managed.

---

#### **5\. The Shedding Ground**

*Where the stag’s old antlers are laid down.*

Somewhere under the southern Antlers, a few days in from the treeline, the Old Stag is said to shed his antlers every spring, and the antlers he sheds to take root. The place is called **the Shedding Ground**, and the few hunters who claim to have seen it describe a grove of low white trees, each branching like a stag’s crown, growing in a ring about a clearing of bare earth.

Shed antler is the one thing in the Elderwood the rite allows to be taken without asking, if it is found lying loose, and the hunters of the Sound sell it, very rarely, at Ek’thalas. A tine of white antler from the Shedding Ground is said to keep a memory as well as a Ledger does, and the Itzarel workshops have bought every piece that has reached them. The Curia has asked them to stop. They have not stopped.

---

#### **6\. The Mending Hollow**

*Where the wounded go, and what it costs them.*

Animals wounded in the Elderwood and let go are said to make their way to one place: a still, deep pool in a fold of ground somewhere in the wood’s heart, ringed with ferns and white stones, where the Old Stag comes to drink. Hunters have followed wounded deer toward it and lost them at its edge. Some of those deer have been seen, a season later, whole.

The story the dying come for is that the **Mending Hollow** heals people too, if they are brought to it and the stag allows them to drink. The story the hunters tell alongside it is that the water takes a memory for every wound it closes, and does not choose which one, and that the people it has healed come out of the Elderwood well, and grateful, and not always sure to whom.

---

#### **7\. The Camp of the Unkept**

*Where the forgotten are supposed to live.*

The Compendium includes this entry because three of its sources asked it to, and because none of them could remember why. Somewhere in the deep Elderwood, they say, the unkept who did not come back have made a camp: a few dozen people of every kind, in houses of bark and stone, who keep no Ledger, wear no beads, have no names and are, in the words of one source, *the happiest people in the west*. It is said that they will take in anyone who finds them, and that nobody who finds them ever wants to leave.

The Balam of Kehir have been looking for it for a long time. They report that they have not found it, and that they would know.

---

### **VI. Along the Edge**

*The Elderwood keeps no towns. The places below are those on its edge that the Daykeepers record, and the landmarks the hunters of the Sound name; nothing further in is given distances, because nobody who has measured them has kept the figures.*

The Elderwood fills the land west of the Sound and the Gulf’s western arm, from the fjord-folk’s villages below the Hushwood in the north to the trail from Ek’thalas to Tsinab in the south, and runs on westward past the edge of the chart. It is bounded on the east by the villages of the Sound and the trails from Kaynab down to Ek’thalas, and is entered, lawfully, only at Kehir. The villages of the Sound, which hunt its edge, are described with the realm’s gazetteer.

**Kehir** — *The Asking Post.*  
The realm’s only post on the Elderwood’s edge, a day’s walk west of the Long Trail where it comes down to Ek’thalas, at the south-eastern foot of the Antlers. It is a Balam watch-platform built in three old pines above a stockade and a single long house, where a Daykeeper keeps the Grey Ledger and stamps the grey seal on every Ledger that goes in and comes out. The Asking Stone stands at the treeline below it and the Bone-Cairns run north and south from the Stone. A detachment of the Balam from Ek’thalas holds it, a year at a time. The present Daykeeper, **Caluna Lacendar**, has kept the Grey Ledger for nineteen outside years, longer than anyone before her, and is known on the Sound for two habits: she writes every entry twice, once in the Ledger and once on a slate she keeps in her pocket, because the Grey Ledger’s own lines for those who go unkept fade with the rest, and she asks everyone who comes out of the wood the same three questions, in the same order, before she lets them past. She has not told anyone what the third question is for.

**The Antlers** — *The White Mountains.* The glaciated massif under which the Elderwood lies, seen to the north-west from Ek’thalas.

**The Shedding Ground** — *The Stag’s Grove.* A ring of antler-shaped white trees under the southern Antlers, where the Old Stag sheds.

**The Mending Hollow** — *The Still Pool.* The pool in the wood’s heart where the wounded are said to be healed, at a price.

### **VII. Advice to the Traveller**

*A marginal note, found in a copy of this entry kept at Kehir. The hand is the same throughout; the ink is not.*

* “Ask at the Stone. Say what you want. Take only that.”

* “Get both grey seals. The first one is for the Balam. The second one is for you.”

* “Tie a cord at the treeline and knot it every hour. If you find a cord on your wrist with knots in it, start back now.”

* “Bring the bones back. Bring them back even if you did not eat the meat. Bring them back even if you are not sure what they were.”

* “If you hear wasps where there should not be wasps, stop walking and lower your eyes and wait. He is only looking.”

* “Do not try to carry anyone to the Hollow. If they are meant to go, he will come for them. If he does not come, they were not.”

* “Write down who you are before you go in. Keep it somewhere you will look.”

*Thus ends the entry on the Elderwood. For the Sound and its villages, see the realm’s gazetteer; for the tree that the Elderwood is not, see the entries that precede this one.*
