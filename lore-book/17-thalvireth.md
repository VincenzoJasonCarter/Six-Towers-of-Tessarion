# 🍃 Thal’Vireth

**Thal'vireth**

*The Grove That Remembers, The Realm That Farms Time*

There is a saying among the Daykeepers, taught to every child before they can properly climb a root-stair: *“Spend your years as you would spend another’s — because one day, you will.”*

Wrapped around the roots of the Verdant Tower in the whispering western forests, Thal'vireth is the only nation in Tessarion built openly around the management of time itself. Where other realms trade in metal, grain, or blood, Thal'vireth trades in something no one else has learned to farm: the memory of its own dead, spent carefully across six districts that each move at a different pace of life.

# **🌳 Overview**

Population: **\~140,000 souls** (est.), concentrated along the root-roads and canopy-walks encircling the Verdant Tower. Precise census figures beyond the outer districts are not maintained — or not released.

●        **Elves (\~78%).** The tree's stewards by treaty and by blood. Life expectancy and pace of life vary enormously by district — a fast-zone farmer and a slow-zone archivist may be the same age and look nothing alike.

●        **Humans (\~10%).** Mostly chronoflow clients, licensed alchemists, and a small resident population of Crownweave-trained mages permitted to study under the Ah K'in. Most live in Sakbeleth, rarely deeper.

●        **Half-elves & other kin (\~7%).** Descendants of unions formed along the trade roads, occupying an uneasy status: legally elven, culturally caught between fast and slow.

●        **Others (\~5%).** Dwarven smiths contracted for Balam armaments, tiefling brokers working the Sakbeleth exchange houses, and the occasional Sangrith “consultant” nobody admits to hosting.

The figure counts only those who keep the Count, which is to say those who live inside the six districts and are written into the Daykeepers' ledgers. The realm's *claim* runs much further than its census. The Desertborn clans of the south, the fjord-folk of the west coast and the Balam outposts in the northern pines are not counted, and in the case of the Desertborn, would object to being.

# **🗺️ The Lay of the Western Reach**

Thal'vireth claims the whole western edge of the known world: a long, broken coast that runs from glaciers in the north to open desert in the south, larger on any map than Crownweave and its five Weaves together. Almost all of it is empty. The realm proper, the six districts and the Tower they ring, fills only the forest at its heart, and the elves speak of everything beyond the last Balam drum-tower simply as *the Outer Reach*.

Travellers who arrive by sea from Crownweave, thirty-odd days west across open water, see the land in roughly this order:

●        **The Quiet Gulf.** A great inland sea that cuts the western land nearly in two, its water still and green, its few islands uninhabited by law. Ships from the east sail into it to reach the realm, because the realm's only harbour faces it.

●        **Nabireth, the Landing.** The harbour-town at the head of the Gulf, on the eastern edge of the forest, and the seaward end of the White Road. Every foreign ship, cargo and visitor comes ashore here and nowhere else. It is administered as part of Sakbeleth and keeps true time.

●        **The Verdant Heartland.** Behind Nabireth the forest rises: tall, wet, seasonal woodland threaded by slow brown rivers, the canopy so high and so continuous that whole districts are built in it rather than under it. The Verdant Tower and Ix'kaanil stand at its centre, and the six districts ring them. Nothing in the ring is more than a week's walk from the Tower, and nothing in it keeps the same hour as its neighbour.

●        **The Witzaran Heights.** North of the forest the ground climbs straight into a wall of glaciated mountains, the Witzaran, and the far side of them is the Obsidian Crown's. The Balam keep a drum-tower, Xamanir, at the foot of the ice and do not go higher.

●        **The Sound and the Hushwood.** West of the forest, two long arms of the sea almost cut the land in two: **the Sound**, running down from the northern ocean, and the Gulf's own western arm reaching up to meet it, with only a narrow neck of land between them, which the Long Trail crosses. The Balam port of **Ek'thalas** stands on the Gulf's arm, and fishing villages line the Sound. North of them lies the dark pine country of **the Hushwood**, and west of all of it, under the white mountains the fjord-folk call the Antlers, lies **the Elderwood**, the old forest that the tree never took, which runs on past the edge of every chart the Compendium holds. Ek'thalas's sailors navigate by stars no other harbour bothers to name.

●        **The Gulf Islands.** A handful of islands in the Quiet Gulf, south of the forest. They are uninhabited by law, and Balam boats from the south drum-tower, Noholir, make sure of it.

●        **K'inamar, the Sunward Reach.** South of the Gulf the forest thins into savanna and then into hot, red desert that runs on to the borders of the Iron Hordes. This is Desertborn country (see *Beyond the Ring*, below): claimed by the Curia, crossed by the Balam, governed by no one.

The roads are few and deliberate. The **White Road** runs dead straight from the quays of Nabireth to the Canopy Court, some ten days' walk, fraying as it goes into foot-trails that peel off toward the villages on either side; the **Root-Road** circles the Tower and links the districts to one another through Count-gates; the **Granary Road** carries the Green Cradle's harvest from the Threshing Mounds down to the Landing; and trails run out through the canopy to the four drum-towers. From the west drum-tower the **Long Trail** leaves the forest, crosses the neck of land between the Sound and the Gulf to Ek'thalas, and runs south the length of the Sunward Reach, where it stops being a road and becomes an argument about where the road used to be. Past the Reach it goes on, as the Flame road, into the lands of the Iron Hordes.

# **🌱 Origins : The Grief That Grew a Tree**

The Verdant Tower predates the elves' oldest remembered seasons — a fact confirmed by their own treaty language, which states plainly that authorship of the Tower remains undetermined by law. What elven history *does* claim is what happened beneath it.

When the earliest wars bled the western forest, soul-stuff pooled beneath the dead the way it pooled beneath every battlefield in Tessarion — hardening into soulstone, veined green with elven memory. Other peoples learned to cut such crystals and drink its power. The first elves refused. To cut a memory, they held, is to murder it twice.

Instead, the woman later remembered only as **the First Keeper** gathered the largest shard — dense with the memory of everyone the forest had lost — and buried it among the Tower's roots rather than sell it. It did not stay a crystal. Fed on so much unspent life, it grew. The living wood did not encroach on the pale stone of the Tower; it grew *through* it, the way the Threadmint's own sealed archive describes: seamless, unreconciled, load-bearing. The elves named what grew **Ix'kaanil**, She of the Sky, and built their nation in its shade.

This is also, the priestesses teach, why the ground beneath Ix'kaanil does not keep ordinary time. A tree that feeds on stored memory does not merely hold the past — it *spends* it, unevenly, the way any inheritance is spent unevenly by the people who receive it. Where the tree pours memory outward fast, the present hurries to catch up. Where it holds memory close, the present is made to wait. The elves did not invent time magic. They grew a tree that eats the dead, and Time Zones are what that appetite looks like from the outside.

*“Memory is weight. Weight, if left unbound, reshapes the ground.” — attributed to the First Keeper, and again, centuries later, to a Threadmint archivist who never explained how she came to know the phrase.*

# **🌳 The Tower : Ix'kaanil**

At the realm's absolute center stands Ix'kaanil, fused seamlessly with the Verdant Tower beneath it — pale stone threaded with living, load-bearing roots. No foundation trench or growth ring has ever reconciled with another; the interior is said to realign subtly between sanctioned visits, an effect the priestesses attribute, officially, to “ambient verdant saturation,” and unofficially to nothing they will discuss with outsiders.

***The Sanctum Root***

The innermost chamber, open only to the High Priestess and those she personally escorts. Here the oldest soulstone deposits lie undisturbed, and here, it is said, a visitor can lose their sense of which year they are standing in.

***The Canopy Court***

A ring of stepped, wood-grown shrines just beneath the tree's lower boughs, where the Ah K'in receive petitioners, hear disputes between districts, and formally grant or deny chronoflow assent.

***The Hollow Archive***

A chamber of preserved bark-glyph records maintained jointly with the Threadmint Library of Crownweave — though Thal'vireth's own copies are considerably less redacted than the ones the Threadmint permits outsiders to read.

**Hazard, acknowledged and unaddressed.** Unshielded time near the Tower's lower chambers has produced temporal dislocation, identity-echoing, and a slow detachment from the present in those who linger. Ix'kaanil does not repel intruders, bar passage, or issue any warning before it does this to them — a fact the priestesses regard less as a flaw than as a kind of honesty.

# **⏳ The Count : How Time Is Kept**

Every elf of Thal'vireth will tell you that the realm does not *have* time zones. It has the Count: one ledger of life, kept by the tree, spent at different rates in different places. The districts are only where the spending happens.

## **The Pace of the Districts**

The Daykeepers publish the pace of each district as a ratio against *outside time*, the hour kept at sea, in Crownweave and on the White Road. The figures are averages. The tree does not keep them exactly, and the closer one stands to the Tower, the less exactly it keeps them.

| District | Pace | One outside year is, inside… |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Iximvael, the Green Cradle | Fast | about six years |
| Itzarel, the Quickening Wards | Fast | about four years |
| Sakbeleth, the White Road | True | one year, by design |
| Balamir, the Jaguar Reach | True, drifting at its inner edge | one year, give or take a season |
| Baktuniel, the Deep Count | Slow | about a month at the outer stacks; a few days in the heartwood |
| Xibaloth, the Broken Deep | Wrong | not published |

A farmer of the Green Cradle who works there for ten outside years has lived sixty. An archivist of the Deep Count who keeps the heartwood stacks for a century of outside time may have aged less than a year. The two may have been born on the same morning.

## **The Two Ages**

Every elf of the realm therefore has two ages, and the Daykeepers record both in the **Ledger of Hours**: their *outside years*, counted from birth by the sea-calendar, and their *lived years*, counted district by district as they move through the ring. Thal'vireth law reckons by lived years. A child raised in the Green Cradle comes of age long before a cousin born the same day in the Deep Count; a Quickening Wards inventor may inherit from a grandmother who is, to look at, younger than she is.

The Ledger is kept twice. Every citizen carries their own: a slim book of bark-paper, opened at birth and stamped at every Count-gate they pass. And every stamp is copied, by the Daykeeper who makes it, into the **great register** in the Heartwood Stacks at Tun'iel, where the realm's whole Count is kept in one place, in the slowest ground there is. The two copies are meant to agree. Where they do not, the Daykeepers decide which one is wrong.

Most of the realm's quieter litigation is about this. Inheritance, betrothal, debt and sentence are all measured in lived years, and the Daykeepers who keep the Ledger are the only people who can say, with authority, how old anyone is. It is not hard to see why the Ledger is where the realm's corruption lives.

## **Count-Gates**

Where a Root-Road crosses from one district into another, it passes through a **Count-gate**: a narrow arch of grown wood with a Daykeeper's post beside it, where travellers' names are entered and their Ledger is stamped with the hour of crossing. Passing between districts without a stamp is not itself a crime. It is merely a way of losing years that no court will afterwards agree you lived.

## **Count-Sickness**

Crossing between a fast and a slow district in a single day is unpleasant, and outsiders feel it worst. The symptoms are known collectively as **Count-sickness**: nausea, a sense of having already heard what is being said, words arriving a beat after the thought, and, in bad cases, a day or two that the sufferer cannot afterwards account for. It passes with rest at true time, which is what Sakbeleth's re-acclimation houses sell. Elves raised in the ring hardly feel it. Elves raised outside it, such as the Skyloom diaspora, feel it as badly as humans and are rarely allowed to forget it.

## **Count-Beads**

Every citizen wears a cord of **Count-beads**, one bead of jade for each lived decade, coloured by the district it was lived in: green for the Cradle, amber for the Wards, white for the Road, black for the Deep Count, red for Balamir. Xibaloth has no colour. A bead for a decade served in the Broken Deep is left blank, and is the one bead nobody asks about.

## **Returning to the Count**

Thal'vireth does not bury its dead. A body is carried to the nearest root-bed of Ix'kaanil and **planted**: laid in the open soil among the tree's surface roots, without a stone or a name, and left for the tree to take. The rite is called *returning to the Count*, and what the dead return is understood quite literally to be the time they will spend, through the tree, on everyone still living. To refuse the rite is to keep your years from your own grandchildren. The Desertborn refuse it.

# **🌿 The Districts**

Six districts ring the Tower, each a distinct pace of life rather than merely a place. Fast districts produce and burn bright; slow districts preserve and wait; neutral districts connect the two — and connect Thal'vireth to the rest of Tessarion.

Seen from above, the ring is lopsided. Sakbeleth runs east from the Tower to the Gulf, the one road out, and the Green Cradle's terraces spread on either side of it. The Quickening Wards lie on the sunlit south, between the Tower and the Gulf shore. The Deep Count holds the shaded north, up to the ice, and the Broken Deep opens on the west, where the Tower's roots break the surface. Balamir wraps the whole of it in the outer canopy like a fence of branches.

## **Iximvael, The Green Cradle — Fast / Agriculture**

Terraced fields and orchard-groves where a season passes in weeks. Thal'vireth's breadbasket, and its most honored form of short life: farmers here often live sixty full years in a decade of outside time, and are remembered as those who “fed the Count.”

**What it looks like.** East of the Tower, on both sides of the White Road, the forest gives way to light. The low hills are cut into terraces hundreds of steps deep, planted with maize, beans, squash and cacao, and from the Root-Road the fields seem to *move*: a terrace green at dawn is gold by the following week, and the outside eye can watch a crop turn without needing to be patient. Orchard-groves of jade-wood and fruit trees line the upper terraces, bending under harvests that come round four and five times in an outside year.

**How it lives.** Farm households are large, quick and young. A Cradle farmer is born, works, marries, raises children and is planted in the time it takes a Deep Count archivist to finish a single catalogue, and the Cradle knows it. Its people are generous, loud, impatient with ceremony and fiercely proud of feeding a realm that, from their side of the gate, barely seems to move. A Cradle family can name eleven generations within a single outside century.

**Places of note.**

●        ***The Hundred Terraces.*** The great stepped hillside north of the White Road, a few days out from the Tower, worked continuously since before the Compact. Each terrace is held by one family line, and its name is cut into the retaining wall; some walls are carved so deep with names that the stone has been replaced twice.

●        ***The Threshing Mounds.*** Long earth platforms at the foot of the terraces where the harvest is threshed, weighed and stamped by Daykeepers before it goes down the Granary Road to Nabireth or up to the Canopy Court as tithe.

●        ***The Planting Field.*** The Cradle's own root-bed, the busiest in the realm. Funerals here are frequent, short and cheerful. Children play in it.

**Custom.** To have *fed the Count* is the highest honour a short life can earn, and a Cradle farmer who works a full sixty lived years has their bead-cord hung on the terrace wall, not planted with them.

**Trouble.** The Cradle feeds the realm and has no voice in it. Its Ah K'in is chosen by the Curia, and a Cradle-born Ah K'in would grow old and die while their own petition was still being read in the Canopy Court. There is a growing feeling on the terraces that a district which lives six times as fast should have six times the say, and a smaller, quieter feeling that it might simply stop sending tithe up the hill and see how long the Deep Count can wait.

## **Itzarel, The Quickening Wards — Fast / Craft & Invention**

A dense workshop-district of alchemists and glyph-engineers whose ideas iterate generations in a mortal lifetime. Politically restless, chronically short-staffed, and the source of most of Thal'vireth's exportable innovation.

**What it looks like.** Itzarel sits on the Tower's southern flank, running down toward the Gulf shore, where the forest floor has been cleared and roofed over with a lattice of grown wood and cloudglass so that the workshops below stand in a permanent green half-light. Smoke from the glyph-forges collects under the lattice and hangs there. The streets are narrow, steep and always being rebuilt; a building that went up in spring is often out of date by summer, and it is common to see a perfectly sound workshop being torn down because something better has been thought of inside it.

**How it lives.** Every workshop is a house, and every house is a lineage. A master's apprentices are, more often than not, their great-grandchildren, and an idea begun by one generation is refined by the next three before the outside world has noticed it exists. Itzarel's work is exported through Sakbeleth: glyph-lamps, root-grown tools, healing tinctures aged to strength in weeks, and the delicate instruments Skyloom's weather-stations buy without asking where they come from.

**Places of note.**

●        ***The Glyph-Forges.*** Long, low furnace-halls where verdant glyphs are cut into wood and stone and set with chips of soulstone dust. The only place in the realm where soulstone is worked at all, and only in quantities the Canopy Court has assented to, by the gram.

●        ***The Patent Wall.*** A long wall of carved panels at the district's heart, where every invention submitted to the Canopy Court for licence is recorded with its inventor's name. Very few of the inventors lived to see their licence granted. The Wall is the Wards' most popular meeting-place and its most bitter joke.

●        ***The Iteration Houses.*** The district's great workshop-lineages, each with its own sign and colours, of which the oldest have been working on the same few problems, unbroken, for longer than Crownweave has had a Charter.

**Custom.** Itzarel dates everything by *iteration*, not by year. To ask a Wards craftsman how old something is invites the answer "fourth iteration", which is to say: three earlier versions, three dead inventors, and no intention of stopping.

**Trouble.** Itzarel invents and the slow districts license. An idea the Wards finish in a lived year can sit before the Canopy Court for an outside decade, which inside the Wards is forty, and the Wards have begun to say aloud the thing every Ah K'in fears: *we invent, they inherit*. Some Iteration Houses have started selling to Crownweave's chronoflow cartels directly, through Sakbeleth brokers, without waiting for assent. Nobody has yet been caught. Nobody has been looking very hard.

## **Baktuniel, The Deep Count — Slow / Preservation**

The sacred heartwood, where time barely moves. Home to the eldest priestesses, the realm's archives, and its most revered relics. Nothing is invented here. Everything is kept. A guardian posted to Baktuniel may outlive the dynasty that posted them.

**What it looks like.** Baktuniel lies on the Tower's shaded northern side and partly *within* it, where Ix'kaanil's roots and the Tower's pale stone have fused into a maze of grown halls. It is quiet in a way that outsiders find hard to describe: not silent, but slow, every sound arriving a little late and leaving later. Dust hangs in the air without falling. Candles burn for weeks. A visitor who watches a leaf drop from the canopy may find, on looking up again, that it has not yet reached the ground.

**How it lives.** The Deep Count is home to the realm's oldest living elves, its archivists, its relic-keepers, and the eldest priestesses of the Curia, together with a small permanent garrison of Balam who are posted here for a few lived years and come home to find their children grown. It is the only district where the passage of outside time is an active concern. Its archivists receive the world's news in heaps, a decade at a time, and spend their careers catching up.

**Places of note.**

●        ***The Heartwood Stacks.*** The realm's archive: corridors of shelving grown out of the living root, holding bark-glyph records older than any surviving outside history. The outer stacks keep about a month per outside year. The inner stacks keep a few days, and are reached only through a Count-gate that the Balam do not allow to stand open.

●        ***The Waiting Wards.*** The Deep Count's quietest and most lucrative service. Foreigners who are dying of something no one yet knows how to cure (nobles, mostly, and the occasional wealthy guildmaster from Crownweave) pay the Curia to be laid in the Wards and kept, at a day or so per outside year, until a cure exists. Some have waited a century. A few cures have come. The Curia does not publish how many patients it has, or how many of their families are still paying.

●        ***The Long Watch.*** The guard-hall at the Deep Count's inner gate, where Balam veterans keep watch over the relics. A Long Watch posting is the most honoured in the Order, and the most feared: you come back to a world that no longer has a place for you.

**Custom.** In the Deep Count, nothing is ever said to be *old*; it is said to be *kept*. Asking an archivist the date is considered faintly rude, rather as asking a stranger their weight would be elsewhere.

**Trouble.** The Waiting Wards are full of people whose estates are being run, in their absence, by heirs who would prefer they never wake. A great deal of quiet money passes from those heirs to the Daykeepers who keep the Wards' ledgers, and a patient's *pace* (how slowly their bed is kept) is a figure that can be changed with a stamp.

## **Xibaloth, The Broken Deep — Slow / Underworld Watch**

The tangled root-caverns descending toward Xibalba, where time does not merely slow but runs wrong. A feared frontier of wardens, exiles, and the occasional criminal sentenced to serve out a debt at a pace meant to feel like punishment rather than mercy. Few volunteer. Fewer return recognizable.

**What it looks like.** West of the Tower, where Ix'kaanil's roots break the surface in a broken country of ravines and sinkholes, they also go down, further than anyone has followed them. The upper caverns are worked: root-stairs, warden-camps, cells cut into the living wood. Below them the passages narrow and twist into the dark, the air turns cold and damp, and time stops behaving as a quantity at all. Lamps burn down in an hour and then burn for a month. Wardens find their own footprints in mud they have not yet walked through.

**How it lives.** Xibaloth has no citizens, only wardens and the sentenced. The Curia sends its worst offenders here, and its most inconvenient ones, with sentences written in lived years. Because the Broken Deep keeps no steady pace, a sentence of ten lived years may end in a single outside season or outlast the judge who passed it. Nobody can tell, going in, which it will be. That uncertainty is the punishment.

**Places of note.**

●        ***The Root-Gate.*** The only sanctioned way down: a single Count-gate in the floor of a root-ravine at Holoth, on the Deep Count's western edge, kept by Balam and logged by Daykeepers who record the hour each warden and prisoner goes in, and then, separately, the hour they come out.

●        ***Hollow Hearth.*** The wardens' camp, a few levels down: barracks, a shrine, a common fire that has not been allowed to go out since the Compact. The Xibaloth Wardens (see *Factions*) meet here, and increasingly decide things here.

●        ***The Debt-Cells.*** Cells grown shut around the sentenced, in passages where the pace is thought to be roughly known. "Thought to be" is the important part.

**Custom.** Wardens in the Broken Deep greet one another not by name but by asking *what hour do you keep?*, and answer with whatever their hourglass shows. Two wardens whose glasses disagree by more than a day are expected to walk back up together until they agree.

**Trouble.** Things come *up* from Xibaloth that the Curia's maps say should not be there: pale, root-fed creatures, wardens who left a lived year ago and return the same morning, and, lately, reports that something below has started keeping a Count of its own.

## **Sakbeleth, The White Road — Neutral / Trade & Passage**

The only district that runs at true, unaltered time — by design. Foreign merchants, diplomats, and chronoflow clients pass through its stone causeways to trade and to re-acclimate before or after exposure to a faster or slower zone. Thal'vireth's closest thing to a Sixfold Quarter, and the district where an outsider is most likely to be welcomed.

**What it looks like.** Sakbeleth is the White Road and everything built along it: a raised causeway of pale, close-fitted stone (*sakbe*, in the old tongue, is simply "white road") that runs for some ten days' walk from the quays of Nabireth on the Gulf, through the forest, to the steps of the Canopy Court. It is the only straight line in Thal'vireth, and the Balam say it is a hair that has been brushed too often. From the Weneleth houses onward, foot-trails peel off it at shallow angles, run beside it a while, then split, and split again, toward the villages of the Green Cradle and the Wards' slopes. The nearer the Road comes to the sea, the more it frays, until from the drum-tower at Lak'inir its last days look less like a road than like a split end. Its stone is cut from the same pale rock as the Tower and is kept at true time by wards renewed every year, so that walking it from end to end feels, to anyone who has crossed a Count-gate, like breathing out.

**How it lives.** Sakbeleth is where the realm meets the world. Its people are traders, brokers, translators, innkeepers, customs Daykeepers and the staff of the re-acclimation houses, a mix of elves, half-elves, humans and the realm's handful of tiefling brokers, and it is the only district where a foreigner can live for years without anyone remarking on their lived age. Everything that enters or leaves Thal'vireth passes through it: Cradle grain going out, Wards instruments going out, Crownweave goods and coin coming in, and the chronoflow contracts that are the Curia's real income.

**Places of note.**

●        ***Nabireth, the Landing.*** The harbour-town at the Road's seaward end, built on piers over the shallows at the head of the Quiet Gulf. Ships from Crownweave make landfall here after thirty-odd days at sea. Customs at Nabireth are run by Daykeepers, and are slower than they need to be.

●        ***The Exchange Houses.*** The great trading halls halfway up the Road, where Crownsblood is changed for Thal'vireth's own jade-tally and where chronoflow contracts (aging, keeping and waiting, each priced by the lived year) are written, witnessed and sealed.

●        ***The Re-acclimation Houses.*** Inns that sell nothing but rest at true time, with dim rooms, plain food and a Daykeeper on hand to correct a guest's Ledger. A night here is the accepted cure for Count-sickness. The good houses are booked out for weeks.

●        ***The Canopy Steps.*** The Road's inland end: a broad stair rising into the lower boughs of Ix'kaanil, at the top of which stands the Canopy Court. Petitioners queue on the Steps for days.

**Custom.** On the White Road, it is good manners to ask a stranger *where they keep their hour* before asking their name, and very bad manners to guess.

**Trouble.** Every illegal thing that enters the realm enters through Sakbeleth, because there is no other way in. The Exchange Houses are where the chronoflow cartels buy influence, where Itzarel's unlicensed work is quietly sold, and where cargo consigned to an Ah K'in by name is waved through without inspection. The Daykeepers at Nabireth know which crates not to open. They are paid not to wonder why.

## **Balamir, The Jaguar Reach — Neutral / Defense**

The high outer boughs and border walkways, home to the Balam warrior-orders. Manages threats from beyond the forest and enforces the High Priestess's time-decrees between districts — including, when necessary, against the districts' own Ah K'in.

**What it looks like.** Balamir is not so much a district as a ring: the uppermost canopy of the forest, around the whole of the realm, walked by suspended bridges, platforms and watch-posts built in the crowns of the oldest trees. It keeps true time at its outer edge. At its inner edge, where its boughs reach over the other districts, the pace drifts: a walkway above the Green Cradle runs a little fast, one above the Deep Count a little slow, and Balam patrols learn to feel the difference underfoot.

**How it lives.** The Balam Order lives here: warrior-lodges, training-grounds, armouries stocked with dwarf-forged steel, and the families of the Order, who are raised above the ground and are proud never to have needed a root-stair. The Order answers to its own war-captain, who answers to the Tree. Balamir also keeps the realm's watch on the Outer Reach, from the Hushwood outposts in the north to the forest's edge above the Sunward Reach in the south.

**Places of note.**

●        ***The Drum-Towers.*** Watch-platforms at every quarter of the ring, each with a great drum that beats the *outside hour*, the true time kept at sea. The drums are how the six districts know what time it really is, and why, on any given day, a drum in Balamir is marking an hour that, several districts away, has not happened yet.

●        ***The Jaguar Lodges.*** The halls of the Balam warrior-orders, one for each of the Order's six lodges, hung with jaguar pelts and the bead-cords of the fallen. Initiation is by a night alone in the canopy above Xibaloth's caverns. Not everyone's night is the same length.

●        ***The Outer Posts.*** Platforms at the forest's edge, facing the Witzaran ice, the Sound and the Sunward Reach. Postings here are long, quiet and dull, until they are not.

**Custom.** A Balam warrior wears their Count-beads on the left wrist, so that the right arm is free. A warrior who has served on the Long Watch in the Deep Count wears a single extra bead of white, and is never asked how long they were gone.

**Trouble.** The Balam Order is sworn to the Tree, not to the High Priestess, and the distinction has never mattered. If the Curia's bond to the Ah K'in ever failed, or if one of them cut their own thread and rooted themselves into the Tree, the Order's war-captain would have to decide, quickly, which of them the Tree had chosen. The captain has already thought about it. The answer is not written down.

# **🌲 Beyond the Ring : The Outer Reach**

Thal'vireth's claim runs to the edges of the western land; its Count stops at the forest's edge. Beyond the last drum-tower, everything keeps outside time, and the people who live there are, in the realm's view, simply *unkept*.

***The Desertborn of K'inamar***

The Sunward Reach is home to the **Desertborn**, clans of elves who left the ring generations ago, in a schism the Hollow Archive does not lend out, and never came back. They refuse the Count, refuse the Ledger, and refuse to be planted: their dead are burned in the open desert so that, as their saying goes, *the tree may eat the sun instead*. They herd, trade and raid across the savanna and the red desert, keep outside time by the stars, and cross the water into Crownweave's Emberweave, where they are known, and trusted, as hard bargainers who have no use for patience. To the Curia they are kin who walked away from their inheritance. To the Desertborn, the Curia are kin who sold theirs.

***The Fjord-Folk***

Along the Sound, small fishing and sealing communities live in the inlets between Ek'thalas and the northern ocean: half-elves, humans and a few elf families who keep outside time and send nothing to the Canopy Court but salt fish. The Balam port is their landlord, customer and protection, and in the long winters it is more or less their government.

***The Hushwood Posts***

In the pine country north of the Sound, the Balam keep a pair of outposts, Siisir and Witzir, against whatever comes down from the north, which is occasionally weather, occasionally beasts, and, more often of late, travellers in grey who say they are surveyors and are not. Witzir faces the Obsidian Crown's border across a few miles of pine. The elves and the Sangrith have never fought a war here. Both sides keep very careful records of why not.

***The Elderwood***

West of the Sound lies the one forest in the west that Ix'kaanil does not keep: **the Elderwood**, *Kehvireth* to the elves, older than the Count and guarded by a white stag the elves call **Sip** and the fjord-folk the Old Stag. Its hours are true, but it forgets: paths, footprints, names, and the ink of any Ledger carried into it, which greys page by page until it cannot be read. The law counts time spent there as no time at all, and to go in meaning to stay until one's Ledger is blank, *going unkept*, is a crime the Balam must stop before it is committed. The hunters of the Sound hunt its edge by an old rite (ask at the Stone, take only what was asked, bring the bones back), which the fjord-folk say goes back to a bargain between the stag and the First Keeper: *the dead of the people go to the tree, and the dead of the forest go to the stag.* The realm keeps one post on its edge, Kehir. See *Of The Elderwood*.

***The Iron Hordes Frontier***

Where the Sunward Reach runs east into scrub and hill, it meets the borderlands of the Iron Hordes. The Desertborn trade and fight with the Horde clans as equals; the Curia regards the frontier as Balam business and pretends not to know what the Desertborn get up to there.

# **📍 Gazetteer of the Western Reach**

The settlements below are the towns the Daykeepers record, district by district, together with the outposts and camps of the Outer Reach that the Balam keep track of. Paces are given against outside time. Beside them, some ninety villages and hamlets are kept in the Ledger, each named, in its district's fashion, for what it grows, makes, keeps or watches: *Buulvael* is the bean-field, *Saasarel* the glass-works, *Tsiibiel* the keeping of writing, *Mu'ukir* the strength-watch.

## **Baktuniel, the Deep Count**

●        **Ix'kaanil** — *The Tower and the Tree.* The Verdant Tower and the tree grown through it, with the Canopy Court in its lower boughs and the Sanctum Root beneath. Seat of the High Priestess, and the realm's capital in every sense but the one a census would recognise: about fourteen thousand live in its boughs and at its roots.

●        **Tun'iel** — *The Heartwood Stacks.* The realm's archive, grown out of the living root north of the Tower. A month per outside year at the outer stacks, a few days in the inner.

●        **Wayebiel** — *The Waiting Wards.* Named for the *wayeb*, the nameless days at the end of the old calendar when nothing is begun. The dying are kept here at a day or so per outside year.

## **Iximvael, the Green Cradle**

●        **Kolvael** — *The Hundred Terraces.* The great terraced hillside north of the White Road, and the Cradle's largest town. About six lived years to one outside year.

●        **Chaakvael** — *The Threshing Mounds.* Where the harvest is weighed and stamped, and where the Granary Road begins.

●        **Kabvael** — *The Honey-Groves.* An orchard village on the Granary Road, famous for the honey of stingless forest bees.

## **Itzarel, the Quickening Wards**

●        **Pak'arel** — *The Patent Wall.* The Wards' heart, with the Wall and the oldest Iteration Houses. About four lived years to one outside year.

●        **K'ak'arel** — *The Glyph-Forges.* The furnace-halls under the cloudglass lattice; the only place in the realm where soulstone is worked.

## **Xibaloth, the Broken Deep**

●        **Holoth** — *Hollow Hearth and the Root-Gate.* The wardens' camp at the head of the only sanctioned way down. Its pace below is not published.

## **Sakbeleth, the White Road**

●        **Nabireth** — *The Landing.* The only legal harbour, at the head of the Quiet Gulf; the White Road's seaward end. The realm's largest town by the outside count, at about eighteen thousand.

●        **Koneleth** — *The Exchange Houses.* Halfway up the Road; Crownsblood changed for jade-tally, chronoflow contracts sealed.

●        **Weneleth** — *The Re-acclimation Houses.* Near the Road's inland end, a few days below the Canopy Steps. Its name means, more or less, "sleeping place".

## **Balamir, the Jaguar Reach**

●        **Xamanir**, **Lak'inir**, **Noholir** and **Chik'inir** — *The Drum-Towers,* north, east, south and west, named for the four quarters in the old tongue. Xamanir faces the Witzaran ice; Lak'inir the Gulf shore north of Nabireth; Noholir the Gulf Islands; Chik'inir the head of the Long Trail.

## **The Sound**

●        **Ek'thalas** — *The Star-Port.* The Balam port on the Gulf's western arm, and the fjord-folk's landlord.

●        **Kaynab** — *Fish-Water.* The largest fishing village on the Sound.

## **The Hushwood**

●        **Xooknab** — *Shark-Water.* A sealing hamlet at the Sound's northern mouth.

●        **Siisir** and **Witzir** — *The Hushwood Posts.* Balam outposts in the northern pines; Witzir faces the Obsidian Crown.

## **The Elderwood**

The Elderwood keeps no towns and no Count. Beyond its one post, the Daykeepers record nothing.

●        **Kehir** — *The Asking Post.* The Balam post at the wood's eastern edge, under the Antlers, where the Grey Ledger is kept and hunters ask leave at the Asking Stone.

## **K'inamar, the Sunward Reach**

The Desertborn name their own places, in the old tongue and without elvish endings, and do not recognise the Curia's map of them.

●        **K'inich** — *Sun-Face.* The Desertborn caravan-market on the Long Trail, and the nearest thing the Reach has to a town.

●        **Ch'e'enha** — *The Wells.* Deep wells in the red desert, held by whichever clan held them last.

●        **Saastun** — *Clear-Stone.* A trading camp on the Flame road near the Iron Hordes frontier.

●        **Muyal** — *Cloud.* A camp in the far south, named for the one cloud a year it is said to get.

# **⚖️ Rule and Intrigue**

## **Layers of Power**

***The High Priestess — Ix Ajaw K'in***

“Lady Lord of Time.” Supreme authority of Thal'vireth and the sole elf permitted unmediated contact with Ix'kaanil's deep root. She alone decides how the tree's memory-flow is allotted across every district — a decision that, quite literally, lengthens or shortens lives.

***The Ah K'in — the Keepers of the Count***

One per district. Each carries a bonded thread of the High Priestess's own connection to the Tower — the **Bond of the Count** — which is the *only* reason an Ah K'in can read or adjust local time-flow at all. The authority is hers, on loan.

●        The bond can be severed instantly, at will, from the Sanctum Root — no trial required. This is the real reason no district has ever successfully broken from the Curia.

●        The bond runs both ways: an Ah K'in faintly feels the High Priestess's health, exhaustion, and doubt. A failing High Priestess is a failing signal, felt by all six Keepers simultaneously.

●        It is held, in whispers rather than doctrine, that a sufficiently desperate or skilled Ah K'in might learn to sever their own thread and root themselves into the Tree independently — becoming the first true rival power the Curia has faced in living memory. No confirmed case exists. That is not the same as no attempts.

***Daykeepers***

Ordinary priest-administrators without the bond — unable to touch time-flow, but responsible for calendars, local disputes, and the daily bureaucracy that keeps six very differently-paced districts legible to one another. Where Thal'vireth's quieter corruptions actually live.

***The Balam Order***

Sworn to the Tree rather than bonded to the High Priestess directly — a separate chain of loyalty answering to its own war-captain. Enforces the Curia's decrees, but is not, strictly, required to.

# **📜 The First Sylvan Compact**

Thal'vireth's foundational treaty with the wider world, recognized jointly by the Elven Accord Seal, the Threadmint Registry, and Loom Council Oversight in Crownweave. Where the Charter of the Six binds Crownweave's peoples to coexist within one city, the Compact binds Thal'vireth to something narrower and stranger:

●        **Elven stewardship of the Verdant Tower is recognized by treaty.** Authorship of the Tower itself remains, by law, undetermined — a distinction the elves insist is intentional.

●        **Extraction of Verdant Soulstone requires elven assent,** case by case, granted only through the Canopy Court. No standing quota exists; every foreign contract is a fresh negotiation.

●        **The Tower is not recognized, under the Compact, as a weapon, a gate, or a conscious intelligence.** These are stated as legal facts. Whether they are also true facts is a separate question the treaty does not ask.

●        **Access to the Hollow Archive beyond the licensed excerpt is deferred by default.** Every petition for expanded study is logged. As of this writing, all remain deferred.

It is this Compact, more than any army, that has kept Crownweave's chronoflow cartels from simply taking what they want. It is also, quietly, the leash the High Priestess uses to justify saying no to her own people.

# **🗡️ Law and Order**

***The Balam Order***

Jaguar-marked warriors stationed through Balamir and along every road into the deeper districts. Discipline is total; corruption is rare but not unheard of, particularly among Balam posted to Xibaloth for long stretches.

***Law in Play***

●        **Open weapons.** Tolerated in Sakbeleth and Balamir; restricted approaching the Canopy Court.

●        **Chronoflow magic.** Any attempt to alter time-flow outside a sanctioned Ah K'in ritual is treated as an act of war against the district it's performed in — not hyperbole, but literal legal classification.

●        **Foreign mages.** Human mages attuned to Verdant Soulstone are watched closely; their subtle time-sense makes them uniquely capable of noticing what the priestesses would rather they didn't.

# **🪶 Factions and Hidden Hands**

***The High Priestess's Curia***

**Goal.** Preserve control of the assent pipeline; keep chronoflow revenue flowing without ever selling Verdant Soulstone outright. **Pressure.** Cannot keep refusing Crownweave's biggest offers forever without risking the Compact itself.

***The Threadmint Loyalists***

**Goal.** Keep the Hollow Archive sealed — from outsiders, and increasingly, from their own Curia. **Pressure.** Openly at odds with Curia elves who want the archive leveraged for better foreign terms.

***The Skyloom Elves***

**Goal.** The Crownweave-resident diaspora — diplomats and mages with estates in Skyloom — want trade normalized and Thal'vireth treated as a full partner in Tessarion's economy, not a sealed archive with a garden attached. **Pressure.** Viewed by homeland elves as soft, assimilated, and possibly complicit in quiet smuggling.

***The Xibaloth Wardens***

**Goal.** Varies by member — some seek release from their sentence; others have been changed by the broken time-flow below and no longer entirely serve the Curia's interests. **Pressure.** A frontier faction the High Priestess can no longer fully account for.

***The Chronoflow Cartels***

**Goal.** Crownweave merchant houses built on legal chronoflow contracts, pushing to loosen or bypass the assent-permit system entirely. **Pressure.** The first house to bribe an Ah K'in instead of petitioning the Canopy Court directly may be the spark a whole arc is built on.

# **✨ Faith & Symbols**

Thal'vireth is united less by a single god than by a single ledger. The tree does not merely grant time — the elves believe it *repays* it, measure for measure, in the same currency a life was spent giving. The old teaching story, **The Mirror That Drinks**, is told to every child: give freely, and your years run full and quick; take without giving, and the tree may simply stop you mid-breath for longer than you meant to lose.

●        **Seal of Thal'vireth:** a stylized root-and-canopy encircling a single unblinking eye — the Tower's watching, unwarning gaze.

●        **Common saying:** *“Spend your years as you would spend another's — because one day, you will.”*

●        **Sacred motif:** jade, quetzal-feather textiles, and the jaguar of the Balam — elite among elves who most resemble their orc and human counterparts in temperament, and are quietly regarded as such.

# **🍵 Daily Life Under the Canopy**

***Food.*** Maize in every form (flatbread, porridge, the thick drink called *sa'*), beans, squash, honey from stingless forest bees, and fruit the outside world has no names for. Cacao is the realm's luxury: drunk bitter and frothed, flavoured with chili or flower-water, and served to guests as a matter of courtesy. A cup of cacao offered in Sakbeleth means *you are welcome*. A cup refused means you have just been very rude.

***Dress.*** Light cotton and bark-cloth, dyed in forest colours, with Count-beads worn openly at the throat or wrist. Quetzal feathers are reserved to the Ah K'in and the High Priestess. Jaguar pelt is reserved to the Balam. A foreigner who wears either, even as a curio bought in Crownweave, will be stopped on the White Road and asked, politely, to take it off.

***Names.*** Elves of the ring carry a birth-name and a *day-name*, the latter given by a Daykeeper from the calendar of the day they were born and recorded in the Ledger. The day-name is used at law and in prayer; friends use the birth-name. Desertborn elves have given up day-names, which is one of the ways the ring recognises them.

***Speech.*** The elves of the ring speak their own tongue at home and the trade-tongue of Crownweave on the White Road, and their place-names are built the same way: an old root, from the speech the realm used before the Compact, joined to an elvish ending. # **🧭 Advice to the Traveller**

●        **Come by sea, and land at Nabireth.** There is no other legal way in, and the Balam who watch the Outer Reach do not distinguish politely between smugglers and the lost.

●        **Get your Ledger stamped.** Every Count-gate, every time. The years you cannot prove you lived are, in Thal'vireth law, years you did not live.

●        **Rest on the White Road.** Spend a night at true time before going deeper, and another on the way out. Count-sickness is not dangerous, but it is very embarrassing in front of an Ah K'in.

●        **Do not hurry anyone in the Deep Count, or slow anyone down in the Cradle.** Both will be offended, and both will be right.

●        **Do not buy soulstone.** Anything offered to you as Verdant Soulstone outside the Canopy Court's assent is either stolen, fake, or a test, and the penalty for all three is the same.

●        **Do not ask a Balam how long they were gone.**

●        **Do not hunt west of the Sound without asking.** Ask at the Stone, take only what you asked for, and bring the bones back.

●        **Mind whose Count you are spending.** Every chronoflow contract has two parties and a tree. The tree always collects.

# **🌿 The Sound of the Forest**

On any given day, Thal'vireth is: the low creak of root-stairs shifting underfoot in Baktuniel, market-callers in Sakbeleth switching fluidly between elvish and the trade-tongue of Crownweave, an Itzarel alchemist arguing that this *particular* prototype will absolutely not need three more years of testing, and somewhere in Balamir, a watch-drum marking an hour that, several districts away, has not happened yet.

*“Welcome to Thal'vireth, traveler. Mind your years — and if you're wise, mind whose Count you're spending them from.”*
