# Of Stormwake, the Open Harbor of the East

## **Of Stormwake, the Open Harbor of the East**

*Excerpt from The Crownweave Compendium, Fourth Roadwarden’s Edition*  
 Shelfmark: CW–ST–03, “On Tides and Foreign Coin”

They say Crownweave was stitched from streets and stories.  
 If that is so, then Stormwake is where the threads loosen—where the pattern opens just enough to let the outside world press its fingers through.

Within the city walls, “Stormwake” names a single salt-smelling quarter of shipping offices and sailors’ shrines. Beyond them, the name belongs to the **eastern prefecture of the Union**, the largest of the Weaves by settlement count: twenty-eight towns, villages, and forts wrapped around a deep, sheltered bay called the **Wakewater** and the long, curling peninsula that encloses it, known everywhere simply as **the Hook**.

Eastward, the Union’s stone gives way to salt-dark quays and wind-worn walls. The air smells of brine, pitch, spice, and strange perfumes that do not belong to any one shore. Masts crowd the skyline like a forest of bare branches, their rigging humming softly even in still weather. Canvas sails—patched, painted, sigiled—hang furled above the docks, each bearing marks of places far beyond Crownweave’s maps.

By day, Stormwake is noise and motion: gulls wheeling, dockhands shouting, chains clanking as cargo is lifted and lowered. By night, it becomes something tighter and sharper—lantern-lit piers, guarded warehouses, quiet deals struck where the water laps close enough to hear but not close enough to interrupt.

If Emberweave is where roads are born, then Stormwake is where roads end—and where stranger ones begin.

---

### **I. The Temper of Stormwake**

Stormwake is a district of tide, trade, and tolerated uncertainty.

Its buildings are practical and layered, raised and rebuilt again and again to answer floods, storms, and changing cargo. Stone bases support timber upperworks; balconies jut over narrow streets to catch sea-breeze and spy on passing rivals. Everything here is designed to be watched, moved, or replaced.

The sea shapes the rhythm of the Weave. High tide brings arrivals, inspections, and a sudden glut of coin. Low tide leaves ships resting crooked in the mud of the Wakewater, tempers fraying as captains wait for water and permission in equal measure.

Stormwake is loud when it needs to be and silent when it must. Prices fluctuate with the tide, rumours with the wind, and loyalties with whatever banner is currently flying highest from a mast.

---

### **II. The Lay of the Wake**

Stormwake faces outward more than any other Weave. It is the only part of the Union from which a ship may sail **north to the Obsidian Crown**, **north-east to Khazrund**, and east along the long coasts toward Emberfall without first rounding the island.

Locals divide the prefecture into four parts:

* **The Harbor Shore** – the northern coast around Caer Veyn, where the great quays, mints, and gun-foundries stand.
* **The Westlands** – the inland country toward Northreach and The Crown, around Marlmond, where foreign cargo is refined, repackaged, and made respectable.
* **The Wakewater** – the inner bay and its western shore, lined with quarantine forts, customs posts, and one very old grudge.
* **The Hook** – the eastern peninsula, walled, fortified, and home to those foreigners who have stopped pretending they will leave.

Its principal routes are:

* by sea, the **Caer Veynese Lane**, which runs from Cleoverbot along the north coast to Caer Veyn and then south down the length of the Wakewater;
* the **Shaky Sun Lane**, which leaves Caer Veyn’s headland due north across open water toward the Obsidian Crown;
* the **Stardust Lane**, branching north-east toward Khazrund;
* the **Serpentine Ebon Route**, the coastal run west to Northreach’s Durdenbeck;
* by land, the **Amethyst Road**, the great highway south-west through the Westlands to The Crown;
* the **Newbomyard Trail**, north-west to Northreach;
* the **Sacred Cleoverbot Trail**, the **Dunscastonese Trail**, and the **Marlney Path**, linking the Harbor Shore’s towns to one another;
* the **Wihulbover Trails** and the **Misty Presterling Path**, running down the Wakewater’s western shore;
* and on the Hook, the **Whirlwind Road** and the **Hartonese** and **Pemavean** trails.

---

### **III. Those Who Call It Home**

Stormwake gathers those who live between ports, not places.

Humans from coastal cities and river-kingdoms make up much of the dockside population: sailors, factors, clerks, and families who have learned to read weather and people with the same wary eye.

Tieflings are common, especially among translators, arcane inspectors, and brokers of “special cargo.” Their familiarity with layered contracts and uncomfortable truths serves them well in a Weave where nothing arrives without complication. Many trace their families to Emberfall ships that arrived generations ago and never quite left.

Dwarves maintain drydocks, warehouses, and bonded vaults, trusting stone and ledger more than tide or promise. Their seals are respected, if not always liked. Many have kin in Northreach and a standing grudge about how rarely those kin visit.

Orcs appear less as clans and more as crews—hired muscle, ship-guards, and enforcers for merchant houses that prefer their warnings delivered clearly and once.

Elves and stranger folk pass through more often than they stay. Stormwake is not unkind to them, merely uninterested in permanence. Sangrith envoys arriving by the Shaky Sun Lane are received with scrupulous courtesy, lodged on the Hook, and watched.

No law binds any people to Stormwake. Yet those comfortable with foreign tongues, shifting ground, and the knowledge that tomorrow’s ally may have sailed by dawn tend to linger.

---

### **IV. Of Trade, Tide, and Thread**

Stormwake is Crownweave’s eastern lung—it breathes ships.

Cargo yards and counting-houses line the inner docks of Caer Veyn, stacked with goods awaiting inspection or resale:

* crates of spice, resin, and preserved fruit,
* barrels of oil, spirits, and alchemical bases,
* bundles of foreign textiles and strange metals,
* wands, staves, and curios sealed with sigils not taught in local academies.

Much of what enters Stormwake is legal by Crownweave’s standards. Some of it becomes legal only after paperwork, inspection, or discreet adjustment—the paperwork in Caer Veyn, the inspection at Stotfordbu, the adjustment in Marlmond.

Arcane items are common, but rarely familiar. Potions brewed for climates warmer or colder than Crownweave’s own circulate beside wands calibrated to traditions no one here fully understands. Buyers are expected to ask questions. Sellers are expected to answer only the ones that matter.

Trade here is governed less by guild price-lists than by origin, risk, and story. An item’s value rises with the danger it survived to reach the dock—and falls sharply if its last owner did not.

#### **Of Foreign Coin**

Stormwake has no coin of its own. It does not need one; it has everyone else’s.

Along the western Harbor Shore and the Westlands stands a chain of small forts and villages known collectively as the **Assay Line**—**Buclester**, **Skipseade**, **Widetonces**, **Newbomyard**, and **Presterling**. Each keeps a Merchant’s Guild charter-house where foreign coin is weighed, tested, and converted at official rates, and where the lighter or stranger pieces are melted and re-struck as Crownsblood. The Assay Line is the reason a Hellglass coin fresh off an Emberfall ship is worth the same in Caer Veyn as it is in The Crown—and the reason a sailor who wants a *better* rate walks past it to a back-room changer on the Hook.

### **V. Caer Veyn, the Harbor City**

*Seat of the Weave, and capital of record of the Union.*

Caer Veyn sits at the north-eastern corner of the Weave, where the Harbor Shore turns south into the Wakewater. It is the Union’s greatest port and, by an old quirk of maritime law, its **capital of record**: every foreign ship’s papers, every treaty sealed at sea, and every charter written for foreign eyes names Caer Veyn, not The Crown, as the seat of the Union. The Crown finds this convenient. It means foreign disputes arrive in Caer Veyn first, and are frequently settled there before anyone in the Hall of the Woven Crown has to hear of them.

The city’s potteries turn out the Union’s shipping jars and amphorae by the thousand, and its ink-works produce **Veyn black**, the ink in which nearly every manifest, bill of lading, and customs seal in the Union is written. Clerks elsewhere in Crownweave will refuse a manifest written in anything else. It is said, not entirely in jest, that Caer Veyn’s true export is paperwork.

#### **1\. The Tidecall Exchange**

*Where cargo becomes claim.*

At the heart of Caer Veyn’s inner harbor lies the Tidecall Exchange: a broad stone concourse built low and wide, sloping gently toward the water. Brass tide-markers are set into the paving at regular intervals, etched with lines and dates recording floods past. Dockhands say the stones remember more storms than any living soul. The highest marker, set near the Exchange’s upper wall, is dated to the year of the Western Gale and is still spoken of with bitterness toward Skyloom.

At high tide, the Exchange is chaos made orderly. Cargo is stacked in deliberate lanes, each marked by chalk sigils and painted boards naming ship, origin, and provisional owner. Factors move quickly between piles, ledgers tucked under one arm, eyes never still. Arcane inspectors—some licensed, some merely tolerated—test seals, tap crates, and mutter notes meant only for their own records.

By low tide, the Exchange empties. What remains are guards, clerks, and the occasional desperate captain arguing over fees that rise as the water falls. It is widely understood that once cargo leaves the Tidecall under seal, its story has officially begun—whatever it was before is no longer the city’s concern.

#### **2\. The Saltbond Vaults**

*Stone remembers what water forgets.*

Set back from the docks behind thick walls of pale granite stand the Saltbond Vaults: bonded warehouses operated under dwarven charter and Crownweave writ. Each vault door bears layered seals—mundane locks, runic wards, and stamped contracts hammered directly into lead plates.

Within, goods await clearance, resale, or quiet disappearance. Entire fortunes have sat untouched here for months, accruing storage fees while their owners argue jurisdiction across three ports and two planes.

The Vaults are neutral ground. Violence within their walls is rare and brief. Those who break the peace find their accounts frozen, their access revoked, and their names circulated among dockmasters with alarming speed.

It is said that if something survives a season in Saltbond without being claimed, it either becomes priceless—or cursed.

#### **3\. Breakwater Row**

*The market that shifts with the tide.*

Running parallel to Caer Veyn’s outer quay, Breakwater Row is less a street than a habit. Stalls are erected at dawn and dismantled before nightfall, their positions dictated by tide, traffic, and mood. No map shows it the same way twice.

Here, one finds the trade Stormwake is best known for:

* foreign potions labeled in three languages and one approximation,
* wands with limited charges and carefully vague instructions,
* charms, catalysts, and reagents unsuited to Crownweave academies but too valuable to discard.

Honest merchants operate beside opportunists and outright frauds. The Weave’s philosophy applies: buyers are expected to test, verify, or accept consequences. Disputes are common, but rarely lethal—bad for business, worse for reputation.

Those who shop Breakwater Row regularly learn to read faces faster than labels.

#### **4\. The Mooring Bell Shrine**

*A prayer before the ropes are cast off.*

Near the easternmost pier stands a simple shrine: a single bronze bell hung from a weathered timber frame, its surface etched with wave and thread motifs. This is the Mooring Bell Shrine, maintained jointly by dockworkers and the Temple of a Thousand Threads.

Sailors, factors, and travelers pause here to:

* ring the bell once for safe arrival,
* twice for safe departure,
* or not at all, if superstition outweighs faith.

Coloured threads are tied to the frame—blue for calm seas, green for profit, red for return. At season’s end, acolytes gather the sun-bleached remnants and burn them, sending their ash into the harbor on the evening tide.

No grand sermons are given here. The sea is considered sermon enough.

#### **5\. The Keel & Knot**

*“Eat well, listen better, leave quietly.”*

A short walk from the Tidecall Exchange, tucked beneath an overhanging warehouse, stands the Keel & Knot Tavern. Its sign—a simple ship’s knot carved in dark wood—swings low enough that tall folk must duck.

Inside, the common room is narrow and long, tables bolted to the floor against sudden surges of patrons or temper. The smell is of fried fish, strong spirits, and damp rope. Conversations overlap in half a dozen tongues, rarely rising above a careful murmur.

The Keel & Knot is favored by:

* ship officers between contracts,
* arcane brokers awaiting clearance,
* and those who prefer their deals overheard only by the tide.

Behind the bar, Old Sera Vant keeps the house’s memory. She does not write anything down, yet seems to know which ships have paid, which have lied, and which should not be served twice in the same night.

In a small alcove near the door rests a thread-altar marked with a wave crossing a crown. Regulars often pause to touch it before leaving—less prayer than habit, but habit counts for much in Stormwake.

### **VI. The Towns of the Wake**

*A gazetteer of the prefecture beyond Caer Veyn.*

#### **The Harbor Shore**

**Dunscaston** and **Whitle** — *The Harbor Guns.*  
North of Caer Veyn, on the headland that guards the harbor mouth, the town of **Dunscaston** casts bronze cannon and the heavier harbor artillery. At the headland’s tip stands **Whitle**, a small citadel-fort with a signal-tower whose fire is the last light of the Union seen by ships bound north on the Shaky Sun Lane—and the first seen by those returning.

**Marlney** — *The Escort Foundry.*  
A town just west of Caer Veyn that casts lighter guns for merchant escorts and ship’s decks. Marlney-cast pieces are stamped with a knot; captains pay extra for them, and pirates pay extra to take them.

**Cleoverbot** — *The Western Harbor.*  
A port on the northern coast where the Serpentine Ebon Route comes in from Northreach. Cleoverbot is older than Caer Veyn and does not let anyone forget it. The **Sacred Cleoverbot Trail** that runs east from its quay is walked each spring by the families of sailors lost at sea, carrying candles from Cleoverbot’s old sea-chapel to the Mooring Bell Shrine. Cleoverbot also blows glass and distils perfumes, much of it for Marlmond.

**Craftry** and **Nostabrid** — *The Shipwrights’ Tools.*  
Two small settlements between Marlmond and Caer Veyn that make the adzes, augers, caulking irons, and rope-hooks of every shipyard in the Union. Craftry holds a town charter out of all proportion to its size; it was granted in gratitude for the tools that rebuilt the harbor after the Western Gale.

**Retrinch** — A fort over the white-sand pits from which Cleoverbot and Marlmond make their glass. It is fortified because white sand, left unguarded, tends to walk.

#### **The Westlands**

**Marlmond** — *The Perfumers’ City.*  
The second city of Stormwake stands inland at the western end of the Weave, where the Amethyst Road sets out for The Crown. Marlmond is where foreign cargo is made respectable. Resins, spices, oils, and aromatics landed in Caer Veyn are brought here to be distilled, blended, rebottled in Marlmond glass, and relabelled as Crownweave goods. Its perfumes are exquisite; its bottling-houses are the reason the Compendium’s entry on customs law runs to eleven pages. Anything that smells of Stormwake in The Crown’s markets probably passed through Marlmond on the way.

**The Assay Line** — **Buclester**, **Skipseade**, **Widetonces**, **Newbomyard**, and **Presterling**. See *Of Foreign Coin*, above. Skipseade and Widetonces also cut the timber for the Wake’s shipyards.

#### **The Wakewater**

**Stotfordbu** — *The Lazaret.*  
A walled citadel on the Wakewater’s eastern shore, with its own temple and a crowded shanty-camp outside its gates. Every ship arriving from beyond the Union’s waters is required to anchor off Stotfordbu until its crew has been examined for plague, curse, and resonance sickness. Crews wait in the camp. Priests of the Thousand Threads tend the sick inside the walls. Most ships are cleared in a tenday. Some are not cleared at all.

**Watford** — *The Seizure Fort.*  
A few hundred paces from Stotfordbu stands a second walled fort, smaller and with a temple of its own. Watford is where arcana confiscated at the Tidecall Exchange are taken to be catalogued, sealed, and **reclassified**. It also has a shanty-camp, though who lives in it, and why they cannot go home, is not a matter of public record.

**Stapton** — A small walled fort between Stotfordbu and the Hook, housing the customs cutters that patrol the bay.

**Bramptontef** and **Pemave** — A farming town and a trading post on the western shore, supplying the forts with food and the waiting crews with drink.

**Wihulbover** — *The Amber Diggings.*  
A walled village south-west of the bay where amber is dug from old sea-beds. Wihulbover amber is cut into jewellery and charms, and occasionally a piece is found with something inside it that nobody can identify. Those pieces go to Watford.

**Hinckburgh** — *The Wakewatch.*  
A hill-city on the southern headland, where the Caer Veynese Lane leaves the Wakewater for the open sea. Hinckburgh’s weather-readers keep a constant watch on the southern and eastern horizons and exchange signals with Skyloom’s Loomwardens—cordially, now, though Hinckburgh has never entirely forgiven the Western Gale either.

**The Wreckers’ Shallows** — The southern reach of the Wakewater, between Hinckburgh and the tip of the Hook, is shallow, shifting, and poorly lit. It is also where Stormwake’s pirates operate. They do not raid the great ships of the Shaky Sun Lane. They wait for the small ones, for the fog, and for a captain in a hurry.

#### **The Hook**

**Nenilton** — *The Strangers’ Shore.*  
A walled town directly across the Wakewater from Caer Veyn, and the largest settlement on the Hook. By long-standing charter, Nenilton is where foreign merchant houses may own warehouses, keep permanent factors, and live under their own customs—within its walls. Sangrith envoys, Emberfall trade-houses, and Khazrund factors all keep halls here. Nenilton’s walls face both ways. Everyone understands why. No one says so.

**Reatonsey** — *The Eastern Bulwark.*  
A walled citadel-town at the easternmost point of the Union, looking out toward Emberfall and Khazrund. Reatonsey sees every ship on the Stardust Lane long before Caer Veyn does, and sends word ahead by signal-fire and fast rider along the Whirlwind Road.

**Knarates** — *The Pirate-Watch.*  
A citadel-fort at the Hook’s southern tip, built to hold the Wreckers’ Shallows. It does not hold them. It does, however, hang the ones it catches where the others can see.

**Alworth**, **Harton**, **Cleoroughes**, and **Albrigh** — The Hook’s smaller towns, trading post, and fort. Alworth and Harton feed the peninsula; Cleoroughes is the Hook’s own market, where the back-room changers do the business the Assay Line will not; Albrigh is a fort whose garrison watches Nenilton at least as closely as it watches the sea.

### **VII. Law on the Waterline**

Stormwake’s law floats between Union and sea.

The **Weave Warden of Stormwake** sits in Caer Veyn and answers to the Loom Council, but enforces authority with a lighter hand than their inland counterparts. Their priorities are simple:

* prevent dockside riots,
* keep the main quays operational,
* ensure foreign incidents do not become international ones.

Beyond that, enforcement is selective. Smuggling is punished only when it disrupts trade. Duels are ignored unless they block traffic. Magical accidents are investigated primarily to determine who will pay for the damage. The Hook is policed by the Warden’s cutters and Albrigh’s garrison; Nenilton, within its walls, is policed largely by itself.

There is one rule understood by all who work the docks:  
 Do not endanger the harbor.

Those who do quickly find themselves without crew, without contracts, or without the ability to dock anywhere that still answers Crownweave’s signals.

### **VIII. Advice to the Traveller**

A marginal note, copied from a damp journal recovered from a wreck in the Wreckers’ Shallows:

* “Never buy magic before you’ve seen the seller watch the tide.”
* “If a vault clerk hesitates before naming a fee, you cannot afford what’s inside.”
* “Stormwake remembers ships better than people. Choose your flag carefully.”
* “Change your coin on the Assay Line. Change it on the Hook only if you know the changer’s mother.”
* “If Stotfordbu holds your ship a second tenday, do not ask why. Ask what they found.”
* “When the harbor goes quiet at midday, leave. Something is about to arrive.”

Thus ends the entry on Stormwake. For records concerning seized arcana, disputed cargos, and the Quiet Reclassifications of the Eastern Reach, see Volume X: *“On Goods That Crossed the Water Twice.”*
