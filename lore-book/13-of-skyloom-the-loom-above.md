# Of Skyloom, the Loom Above

*Excerpt from **The Crownweave Compendium**, Fourth Roadwarden’s Edition*  
 *Shelfmark: CW-SK-01, “On the Western Heights”*

---

### **Of Skyloom, the Loom Above**

Standing in the central squares of Crownweave, if you look west and follow the line of the great avenue until the roofs run out, you will see it: a pale oval hanging above the land, anchored to the earth by towers of stone and rune-light.

This is **Skyloom**—the Weave that never quite stands on the ground it governs.

Within the city walls, “Skyloom” names only the narrow quarter at the foot of the Skyward Towers. Beyond them, the name belongs to the whole **western prefecture of the Union**: twenty-two settlements spread across the green hill-country between The Crown and the western sea, every one of which, in some way, keeps the floating Loom supplied, defended, lit, fed, or paid.

Where Emberweave is the road’s gate and Northreach the iron threshold toward colder lands, Skyloom is **Crownweave’s western sky-frontier**. It faces the open water toward distant Thal’Vireth, watches the winds that roll in off that sea, and bends them, gently, to the Union’s needs.

If The Crown is where crowns speak and Emberweave is where caravans are forged, then **Skyloom is where the air itself is argued with and, more often than not, persuaded.**

---

### **I. The Lay of the Western Heights**

Skyloom is the Union’s most settled prefecture and its most orderly. Its towns sit close together along a web of short roads, few more than a day’s walk apart, so that the land looks less like a province than a single, loosely stitched estate.

It divides, by local habit, into three parts:

* **The Shadowline** – the eastern towns nearest The Crown, over which the Loom’s shadow passes each afternoon. Here stand the anchors, the mints, and the arsenals.
* **The Windward Coast** – the western shore and its heights, facing the sea and whatever blows in from it. Here the harbors, landing-fields, and weather-stations keep watch.
* **The Northern Arm** – the long, narrow spur of land running north to Skyfall Reach, where the Loom sends what it pulls out of the sky.

The principal roads are:

* the **Skyfall Reach Road**, running from the northern tip down the Arm to Windperch, with a branch east toward Northreach’s Iron Crucible;
* the **Sapphire Road**, linking Cloudmoor Harbor, Chadon, and Burbotford—the route along which most instruments, glass, and flight-gear travel;
* the **Old Trail**, cutting inland from Windperch past Gimorand toward the heart of the Shadowline;
* the **Stillmerean Trail** and **Chaillean Trail**, stitching together the southern lake-towns;
* the **Fjerfian Trail**, leaving Central District south-east toward Emberweave.

---

### **II. The Shape of the Loom**

Above the Shadowline floats the Weave’s true heart.

At the eastern edge of the prefecture, where the land rises toward The Crown, stand the **Skyward Towers**—three tall spires rooted firmly in stone. They house the anchor-runes, lift-wards, and harassed clerks whose task it is to make sure the sky-bound portion of Skyloom stays attached to the rest. The town that has grown up around their feet is called, with little imagination, **Central District**.

Above these towers floats the Loom itself: a pale oval of stone and cloudglass, its underside ribbed with faintly glowing sigils, its edges ringed by low walls and open terraces. From the fields below it appears almost serene, drifting just enough to remind nervous newcomers that “anchored” is a matter of degree.

The locals speak of:

* the **Groundring** – the lower level of the floating island, where those without wings can walk, and

* the **Aerialring** – the higher, thinner loop of platforms and galleries reserved mostly for the winged and the very trusted.

From The Crown, one reaches the Loom via the Skyward Towers. From Emberweave and Stormwake, the view of its underbelly on the western horizon serves as a useful reminder that not all danger comes from the sea.

### **III. The Groundring**

The **Groundring** is the part of the Loom most visitors mean when they claim to have “been up there”.

Its northern edge holds the **Skyward Gate Docks**, where skycraft sidle in along glowing mooring-rings. Couriers from Northreach, diplomatic cutters out of Caer Veyn, and merchant vessels that prefer thin air to crowded roads all pass through these docks before descending to the towers or remaining aloft. Heavier craft rarely come this high; they put down instead at Farwatch Landing on the coast, and their cargo comes up by lift.

To the west and south spreads the **Luminous Ward**, a district of pale houses and glass-roofed courtyards where light is treated more as a guest than a resource. Mirrors, thin stone, and cleverly angled panels (most of them fired in Midhurstnes) pull dawn and dusk deep into interior rooms. Wind-chimes and wire-songs hum between balconies, giving the whole ward a faint, constant music.

On the eastern curve lies the **Veil Court**, a broad, reflective plaza open to the sky above and The Crown below. Here, treaties concerning:

* sky-lanes over the Union,
* the extension of weather wards over the other Weaves,
* and the limits of certain magical experiments

are read aloud and bound. A discreet arch at the Court’s edge houses the **Windspire Lift**, an invisible column of rising air that carries those with the proper seals up to the higher tier.

At the Groundring’s centre stands the **Loom Temple**. Open to the sky at its apex, it houses the great ring of woven light from which the Weave takes its name. Loomwardens pace its circumference, adjusting sigils and murmuring complex formulae. From here, winds are redirected over Emberweave’s dust, storms are delayed to spare the northern harvest, and the weather over Caer Veyn’s harbor is kept—barely—within limits the harbormasters can tolerate.

At the eastern tip of the Groundring is **Stormwatch Edge**, a simple stone terrace with a simple stone rail. It overlooks The Crown’s western wall and, beyond it, the slow, silent churning of the **Everstorm** that rings the heart of the Union. Many oaths are taken here; a few are not survived.

---

### **IV. The Aerialring**

If the Groundring belongs to Crownweave, the **Aerialring** belongs to whatever has learned to trust the sky.

Reached via the Windspire Lift or through guarded stairways in the Loom Temple, the Aerialring is a thinner oval of open galleries, high aeries, and **Sky Lanes**—paths in the air marked by dim rune-light, along which winged patrols fly. The lamps that mark the lanes over the countryside below are tended from the ground, by the candle-makers of Aillenestre and Fercelle.

The **High Aeries** on the western and eastern flanks house:

* elves of the upper air,
* aarakocra scouts,
* harpies who have traded shriek for discipline,
* and certain tieflings and Sangrith bearing rune-bound wings.

The northern section holds the **Loft Market**, a compact cluster of stalls selling:

* feathersteel harness and flight-cloaks (Castfield leather, Waringley wool),
* instruments that measure altitude, pressure, and cloud-depth,
* small vials of carefully stored, highly unstable weather.

At the Aerialring’s exact centre lies the **Halo Overlook**, a circular platform of translucent stone suspended directly above the Loom Temple’s inner ring. From here, one can look down through crystal at the Loom itself, and beyond that to the fields of the Shadowline—a faint pattern of roads and threads barely visible through spell-glass.

The Overlook is used sparingly: for flight-rites, public auguries, and vows whose consequences are expected to outlive everyone present.

To the south, the **Dropway** opens as a clear shaft of air toward the hills below. Young fliers are taught, under supervision, precisely when falling becomes flight and when it does not. Those who learn the lesson incorrectly are buried at Buneymouth.

### **V. The Towns Beneath the Loom**

*A gazetteer of the prefecture, arranged by region. Settlements of note are given in full; lesser ones in brief.*

#### **The Shadowline**

**Central District** — *Seat of the Weave.*  
The anchor-town. Its plazas cluster around the roots of the Skyward Towers, and its buildings are low and broad so as not to foul the lift-wards. Every afternoon the Loom’s shadow crosses it end to end; shopkeepers set their clocks by it. Central District is where the Weave Warden holds court, where lift-seals are issued, and where the Loom’s own needs—cheese, liquor, and a great many candles—are gathered before being sent up. Its distilleries produce a clear, cold spirit known as **cloudwine**, drunk mostly by people who have just come down.

**Burbotford** — *The Garrison Ford.*  
The largest town in Skyloom, built around a river crossing and a heavy citadel. Burbotford Keep houses the Weave’s ground-tethered watch and the reserve wings of the Aerialring guard, and its storehouses are the Loom’s pantry of last resort. Bootmakers, cheesewrights, potters, and bronze-smiths crowd its lanes. Locals claim that if the Loom ever falls, it will fall on Burbotford first; the Loomwardens insist this is not true, and have never said where it *would* fall.

**Midhurstnes** — *The Glass Furnaces.*  
A plaza-town of kilns and glasshouses where the Union’s finest **cloudglass** is blown: the thin, pale panes of the Luminous Ward, the crystal of the Halo Overlook, and the lenses of every pressure-gauge sold in the Loft Market. Midhurstnes also produces fine ceramics and a light, faintly ozone-scented perfume that visitors describe as “smelling of weather.”

**Clitford** — *The Lifesap Mint.*  
A small walled village that strikes the Union’s supply of **Lifesap**, the elven coin most associated with Skyloom. Its mint-house is tended by elven assayers who treat each die as a family heirloom. Travellers are not welcome after dark.

**Hinckhamp** — *The Strongbox.*  
A tiny citadel on the road south of Castfield where the Weave’s minted coin is held before transfer to The Crown. It has more guards than residents, and no tavern.

**Waringley, Castfield, and Chaille** — *The Arsenal Belt.*  
Three towns on the southern and eastern roads that between them arm the Weave. **Waringley** casts bronze for anchor-chain fittings and wind-ballistae, and raises the sheep whose wool lines Skyloom’s flight-cloaks. **Castfield**, walled, works the leather and steel of feathersteel harness. **Chaille**, the southernmost settlement in the prefecture, is where new wind-artillery is test-fired out over open water.

**Reaford** — A jeweller’s village whose settings are made to hold crystal steady against vibration; much favoured by fliers.

**Holtondon** and **Burtonrith** — River-mill and timber towns that feed the southern Weave. Burtonrith’s light, close-grained wood goes to skycraft frames; Holtondon’s mills grind grain and turn tools.

#### **The Windward Coast**

**Cloudmoor Harbor** — *Where Keel Meets Cloud.*  
Skyloom’s great western port, and the only harbor in the Union where mast and mooring-ring share a quay. Sea-going ships tie up below, skycraft moor to tall iron masts above, and cargo is swung between them on cranes. Ships from the western sea—including the rare elven vessel out of Thal’Vireth—make first landfall here. Cloudmoor is famous for its boots, harnesses, and preserved provisions, and infamous for **Aurora’s Rest**, a navigators’ inn where charts are spread across every table and a spilled drink can cost more than a ship.

**Farwatch Landing** — *The Ground-Field.*  
A citadel on the coastal highlands with a broad, level landing-field for skycraft too heavy to reach the Loom. Its watch-tower is the westernmost manned post in the Union; on clear days its keepers claim to see the green line of Thal’Vireth. Few believe them.

**Stillmere** — *The Still Water.*  
A lake-port on the south-western shore where, by long-standing agreement, the Loomwardens hold the air **perfectly still**. No wind reaches Stillmere’s mere. It is therefore the only place in the Weave where the most delicate work can be done: lens-grinding, calibration of wind-instruments, casting of bronze anchor-rings, and the test-firing of artillery whose flight must be measured without interference. The silence unsettles visitors. The locals no longer hear it.

**Buneymouth** — *The Larder and the Fallen Field.*  
A market-town that preserves, pickles, and salts much of what the Loom eats. Beyond its northern hedges lies the **Fallen Field**, where those who fell from the Loom—by accident, by misjudgement at the Dropway, or from Stormwatch Edge—are buried. Of late, several of those graves have been found **disturbed from within**. The Weave Warden has classified the matter as “unexplained settling of soil.” Buneymouth’s gravediggers now work in pairs.

**Chadon** and **Fercelle** — Trading posts on the Sapphire Road. Chadon is the clearinghouse for ceramics, boots, and harness bound for the Loft Market; Fercelle makes the long-burning candles that light the Sky Lanes.

#### **The Northern Arm**

**Skyfall Reach** — *Where the Sky Is Put Down.*  
At the northern tip of the prefecture stands Skyloom’s strangest city. When the Loom Temple turns a storm away from the Union, the weather has to go somewhere; much of it is sent north and set down here. Skyfall Reach endures more hail, lightning, and sudden rain in a season than the rest of the Union does in a decade. Its people build accordingly: low, rounded, and grounded with copper.

Two things make the Reach more than a lightning-sink:

* **The Skyfall Grove**, a sacred forest of tall, storm-scarred trees grown under constant weather. Elven Loomwardens tend it as a shrine, and wood taken from it (rarely, and only with permission) is said to remember the storms that shaped it.
* **The Reach Tear**, a standing rift at the edge of the town. The Loomwardens call it a *pressure valve* and send surplus weather into it. What is on the other side has never been formally described.

The Reach also weaves cloth and makes boots—mostly, one suspects, because nothing else wears out quite as fast there.

**Windperch** — *The Aerie Town.*  
A hill-city on the north-western heights and the largest settlement of winged folk outside the Aerialring. Windperch keeps the Weave’s ground weather-stations, trains young fliers in open air before they ever approach the Dropway, and distils a strong liquor considered medicinal after a hard landing.

**Gimorand** — *The Watched Door.*  
A small walled fort on the Old Trail, garrisoned to watch a second rift in the hills to the south—the **Gimorand Tear**. Unlike the Reach Tear, nothing is sent into it. The garrison’s orders are to report anything that comes *out*. The orders do not say what to do next.

**Favantes** and **Aillenestre** — A village and a hamlet that supply Gimorand and Windperch with liquor, provisions, and lamp-candles. Aillenestre is kept by an old elven family who have tended the Sky Lane beacons over the Northern Arm for three generations.

### **VI. The Folk of the Loom**

Skyloom is home to those whose lives are measured in **currents and calculations**.

Elves of the sky, ranging from cloud-pale to storm-dark, provide the bulk of its Loomwardens, ward-engineers, and diplomatic voices, and they are the majority in Central District and the Northern Arm. Their politics, like their spells, are rarely hurried but always aware of pressure. It is no accident that the elven **Lifesap** circulates more freely here than anywhere else in the Union.

Winged peoples cluster on the Aerialring and in Windperch: some native, some from distant eyries or cliff-cities who have found in Skyloom a place where their talents are valued and their anxieties about low ceilings are understood.

Humans here tend toward navigators, astronomers, and chart-makers—people who map not roads but winds. They dominate Cloudmoor Harbor and the southern towns, and maintain close ties to Emberweave’s caravan masters and Stormwake’s ship captains; information flows up and down the Skyward Towers as readily as goods.

Dwarves are most often seen in the Skyward Towers, the Loom Temple, and the foundries of Stillmere and Waringley, where their aptitude for precision work makes them valued anchor-smiths and rune-maintainers, even if they grumble about the height.

There is an unspoken divide between those who can step off a balcony and trust the air to catch them, and those who must trust in lifts, railings, and the Loomwardens’ calculations. The former call the latter *ground-tethered* with absent sympathy; the latter call the former *wind-drunk* in private. Both are, inconveniently, correct.

### **VII. Law and Quiet Precarity**

Skyloom’s law is quieter than Emberweave’s, but no less firm.

The **Weave Warden of Skyloom** is elected from among the senior Loomwardens, chosen for their ability to understand what will happen if a ward fails or a storm is turned the wrong way. The Warden sits in Central District and speaks seldom in the Loom Council, but when they do, people tend to listen; the threat of a badly timed gale can be more persuasive than any speech.

The watch here is thinner on the ground but better trained. In the countryside, the ground-tethered watch rides out of Burbotford Keep. On the Groundring, they patrol the docks, Veil Court, and Loom Temple. On the Aerialring, wing-guards enforce three simple rules:

1. No reckless flight near docks, edges, or the Sky Lane beacons.
2. No tampering with lift, gravity, or wind without proper sigils.
3. No duels over open Everstorm.

Accidents do occur. Most are treated as lessons, a few as crimes. The true taboo is anything that undermines trust in Skyloom’s safety. A Weave will tolerate eccentricity; it will not forgive being made to doubt the integrity of the stones it stands on—or the spells that keep those stones aloft.

### **VIII. Advice to the Traveller**

*A marginal note, preserved from the journal of a Northreach caravan-captain who “went up once and decided the ground was friendlier”:*

* “Do not look down from the Skyward Towers until you have both hands on the rail. The view is less likely to win that way.”
* “In Aurora’s Rest, if someone unrolls a map, do not put your drink on it. Some of those charts cost more than your wagon.”
* “If a Loomwarden says the storm will arrive in three days, plan for two and a half. If they say they cannot move it, believe them.”
* “Do not take the Skyfall Reach Road in the wet season. The Reach does not have a dry season.”
* “In Stillmere, do not whistle.”
* “If you are asked to speak a vow upon the Halo Overlook, remember that the wind up here carries sound further than you think—and back down to the fields you came from.”
