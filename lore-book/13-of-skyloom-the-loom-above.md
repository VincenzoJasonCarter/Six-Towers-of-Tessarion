# Of Skyloom, the Loom Above

*Excerpt from **The Crownweave Compendium**, Fourth Roadwarden’s Edition*  
 *Shelfmark: CW-SK-01, “On the Western Heights”*

---

### **Of Skyloom, the Loom Above**

Standing in the central squares of Crownweave, if you look west and follow the line of the great avenue until the roofs run out, you will see it: a pale oval hanging above the city, anchored to the earth by towers of stone and rune-light.

This is **Skyloom**—the district that never quite stands on the ground it governs.

Where Emberweave is the road’s gate and Northweave the march toward colder lands, Skyloom is **Crownweave’s western sky-frontier**. It watches the winds that roll in from whatever lies beyond the city’s walls and bends them, gently, to the city’s needs.

If The Crown is where crowns speak and Emberweave is where caravans are forged, then **Skyloom is where the air itself is argued with and, more often than not, persuaded.**

---

### **I. The Shape of the Loom**

Skyloom is not a single block of streets, but a **tiered district**.

Beneath, on the western edge of The Crown, stand the **Skyward Towers**—three tall spires rooted firmly in Crownweave’s stone. They house the anchor-runes, lift-wards, and harassed clerks whose task it is to make sure the city’s sky-bound portion stays attached to the rest.

Above these towers floats the true body of Skyloom: a pale oval of stone and cloudglass, its underside ribbed with faintly glowing sigils, its edges ringed by low walls and open terraces. From the streets below it appears almost serene, drifting just enough to remind nervous newcomers that “anchored” is a matter of degree.

The locals simply speak of:

![][image31]

* the **Groundring** – the lower level of the floating island where those without wings can walk, and

![][image32]

* the **Aerialring** – the higher, thinner loop of platforms and galleries reserved mostly for the winged and the very trusted.

From The Crown, one reaches Skyloom via the Skyward Towers; from Emberweave and Stormward, the view of its underbelly serves as a useful reminder that not all danger comes from the horizon.

---

### **II. The Groundring**

The **Groundring** is the part of Skyloom most visitors mean when they claim to have “been up there”.

Its northern edge holds the **Skyward Gate Docks**, where skycraft sidle in along glowing mooring-rings. Couriers from Northweave, diplomatic cutters from Storm, and merchant vessels that prefer thin air to crowded streets all pass through these docks before descending to the towers or remaining aloft.

To the west and south spreads the **Luminous Ward**, a district of pale houses and glass-roofed courtyards where light is treated more as a guest than a resource. Mirrors, thin stone, and cleverly angled panels pull dawn and dusk deep into interior rooms. Wind-chimes and wire-songs hum between balconies, giving the whole ward a faint, constant music.

On the eastern curve lies the **Veil Court**, a broad, reflective plaza open to sky above and city below. Here, treaties concerning:

* sky-lanes over Crownweave,  
* the extension of weather wards around outlying districts,  
* and the limits of certain magical experiments

are read aloud and bound. A discreet arch at the Court’s edge houses the **Windspire Lift**, an invisible column of rising air that carries those with the proper seals up to the higher tier.

At the Groundring’s centre stands the **Loom Temple**. Open to the sky at its apex, it houses the great ring of woven light from which the district takes its name. Loomwardens pace its circumference, adjusting sigils and murmuring complex formulae. From here, winds are redirected over Emberweave’s dust, storms are delayed to spare Northweave’s harvest, and the roaring weather over the Stormward docks is kept—barely—within limits the harbourmasters can tolerate.

At the southern tip of the Groundring is **Stormwatch Edge**, a simple stone terrace with a simple stone rail. It overlooks both Crownweave’s western wall and, far below, the slow, silent churning of the Everstorm that rings the city. Many oaths are taken here; a few are not survived.

---

### **III. The Aerialring**

If the Groundring belongs to Crownweave, the **Aerialring** belongs to whatever has learned to trust the sky.

Reached via the Windspire Lift or through guarded stairways in the Loom Temple, the Aerialring is a thinner oval of open galleries, high aeries, and **Sky Lanes**—paths in the air marked by dim rune-light, along which winged patrols fly.

The **High Aeries** on the western and eastern flanks house:

* elves of the upper air,  
* aarakocra scouts,  
* harpies who have traded shriek for discipline,  
* and certain tieflings and Sangrith bearing rune-bound wings.

The northern section holds the **Loft Market**, a compact cluster of stalls selling:

* feathersteel harness and flight-cloaks,  
* instruments that measure altitude, pressure, and cloud-depth,  
* small vials of carefully stored, highly unstable weather.

At the Aerialring’s exact centre lies the **Halo Overlook**, a circular platform of translucent stone suspended directly above the Loom Temple’s inner ring. From here, one can look down through crystal at the Loom itself and beyond that to the Crown below, a faint pattern of streets and threads barely visible through spell-glass.

The Overlook is used sparingly: for flight-rites, public auguries, and vows whose consequences are expected to outlive everyone present.

To the south, the **Dropway** opens as a clear shaft of air toward the city’s western wall and the hazy glimmer of the Everstorm. Young fliers are taught, under supervision, precisely when falling becomes flight and when it does not.

---

### **IV. The Folk of the Loom**

Skyloom is home to those whose lives are measured in **currents and calculations**.

Elves of the sky, ranging from cloud-pale to storm-dark, provide the bulk of its Loomwardens, ward-engineers, and diplomatic voices. Their politics, like their spells, are rarely hurried but always aware of pressure.

Winged peoples cluster on the Aerialring: some native, some from distant eyries or cliff-cities who have found in Skyloom a place where their talents are valued and their anxieties about low ceilings are understood.

Humans here tend toward navigators, astronomers, and chart-makers—people who map not roads but winds. Many maintain close ties to Emberweave’s caravan masters and Stormward’s ship captains; information flows up and down the Skyward Towers as readily as goods.

Dwarves are most often seen in the Skyward Towers and Loom Temple, where their aptitude for precision work makes them valued anchor-smiths and rune-maintainers, even if they grumble about the height.

There is an unspoken divide between those who can step off a balcony and trust the air to catch them, and those who must trust in lifts, railings, and the Loomwardens’ calculations. The former call the latter *ground-tethered* with absent sympathy; the latter call the former *wind-drunk* in private. Both are, inconveniently, correct.

---

### **V. Law and Quiet Precarity**

Skyloom’s law is quieter than Emberweave’s, but no less firm.

The **Weave Warden of Skyloom** is elected from among the senior Loomwardens, chosen for their ability to understand what will happen if a ward fails or a storm is turned the wrong way. They speak seldom in the Council of The Crown, but when they do, people tend to listen; the threat of a badly timed gale can be more persuasive than any speech.

The watch here is thinner on the ground but better trained. On the Groundring, they patrol docks, Veil Court, and Loom Temple. On the Aerialring, wing-guards enforce three simple rules:

1. No reckless flight near docks or edges.  
2. No tampering with lift, gravity, or wind without proper sigils.  
3. No duels over open Everstorm.

Accidents do occur. Most are treated as lessons, a few as crimes. The true taboo is anything that undermines trust in Skyloom’s safety. A district will tolerate eccentricity; it will not forgive being made to doubt the integrity of the stones it stands on—or the spells that keep those stones aloft.

### **VI. Advice to the Traveller**

*A marginal note, preserved from the journal of a Northweave caravan-captain who “went up once and decided the ground was friendlier”:*

* “Do not look down from the Skyward Towers until you have both hands on the rail. The view is less likely to win that way.”  
* “In Aurora’s Rest, if someone unrolls a map, do not put your drink on it. Some of those charts cost more than your wagon.”  
* “If a Loomwarden says the storm will arrive in three days, plan for two and a half. If they say they cannot move it, believe them.”  
* “If you are asked to speak a vow upon the Halo Overlook, remember that the wind up here carries sound further than you think—and back down to the streets you came from.”
