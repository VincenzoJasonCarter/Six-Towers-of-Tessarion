# Of Emberweave, the Burning Gate of the South

*Excerpt from **The Crownweave Compendium**, Fourth Roadwarden’s Edition*  
 *Shelfmark: CW-EM-01, “On the Southern Approaches”*

---

### **Of Emberweave, the Burning Gate of the South**

They say Crownweave was stitched from streets and stories.  
 If that is so, then **Emberweave** is the place where the cloth frays into dust and road, where the city’s careful weave meets its own scorched edge.

Southward, the stones grow darker and hotter underfoot. The air dries, salted with grit and the smells of spice, horse-sweat, and hot iron. Canvas awnings in sun-faded reds and oranges stretch from wall to wall, filtering daylight into rusted gold. Braziers smoulder by habit more than need—some cooking, some praying, some merely asserting that embers still belong to the living.

By day, Emberweave feels like standing in the mouth of a furnace that has only just begun to open. By night, it becomes a lantern-lit maze of music, dice, murmured bargaining, and the creak of wagons being readied for dawn.

If The Crown is where crowns speak, and Skyloom is where winds are woven, then **Emberweave is where caravans are born, broken, measured, and sent back out again.**

Within the city walls, “Emberweave” names the southern quarter described below. Beyond them, the name belongs to the **southern prefecture of the Union**: the broadest of the Weaves, thirty-six towns, villages, and forts scattered across hot river-country, a dry western tableland, and a long coast facing the Iron Hordes across the water. It is governed from the river-port of **Fjerfi**; its towns are described in the gazetteer that follows the quarter’s own entry.

---

### **I. The Temper of Emberweave**

Emberweave is a district of **heat, horizon, and hurry**.

Roofs are flat and close-packed, built not for beauty but to shrug off merciless sun and catch what little night breeze dares to creep in from the wastes.

Streets run in practical lines toward gates and plazas, then dissolve into knots of alley where stalls, lean-tos, and temporary camps crowd together in the certainty that nothing here is truly permanent. Dust gets everywhere; those who cannot tolerate it do not stay long.

It is not a subtle place. Prices, tempers, and stakes run high and close to the surface. Yet beneath the shouting and the sweat there is a shared understanding: without Emberweave, much of Crownweave’s wealth would never arrive—and many of its problems would never leave.

---

### **II. Those Who Call It Home**

Emberweave gathers those whose lives are measured in **roads walked, not walls built**.

Humans of the southern marches, border towns, and desert-edge cities form much of its settled core. Their families keep taverns, stables, wayhouses, and warehouses, always with one eye on the horizon and another on their ledgers.

Orcs are strongly present. Clan warriors, caravan guards, outriders, and envoys from distant strongholds anchor the district with banners and lodges. Their reputation keeps a certain order: one does not start trouble lightly where so many contracts are backed by orcish honour.

Tieflings find ready work as translators, contract-scribes, and quiet brokers of goods that “mislaid their papers” somewhere along the road. They excel at knowing which questions not to ask.

Dwarves tend forges and waystations, fitting new bands to wheels, mending axles and arms, and keeping immaculate books on debts incurred under their roofs. In a district where a promise can be more valuable than a purse, dwarven accounting is a form of magic all its own.

Elves and Sangrith are fewer but far from rare. Desertborn elf clans move through Emberweave with the ease of those already used to long, hard light. Sangrith oath-binders and those who deal with them find fertile ground in a place where survival and sworn word walk very close together.

No edict binds any race to Emberweave. Yet those most at ease with dust on their boots and steel at their back find their way here, as if drawn by some dry, persistent wind.

---

### **III. Of Trade, Thread, and Road**

Emberweave is **Crownweave’s southern lung**—it breathes caravans.

In its broad squares, wagons are assembled in uncertain lines. Pack-animals stamp and snort, harness rattles, and the air buzzes with last-minute inventories and arguments.

Warehouses nearest the main routes hold the essentials of long travel:

* dried meat, hard bread, and grain,  
* coils of rope, tar, and spare wheel-bands,  
* tarps, tents, packs, saddles, and harness,  
* lamp oil, flint, torches,  
* and above all, **water**, measured more carefully than gold.

Mercenary captains and caravan masters haggle over:

* which routes are passable this season,  
* where raiders or monsters were last seen,  
* what hazard pay will keep blades loyal and eyes open in the dark.

Here, prices are set less by guild decree than by **rumour, weather, and memory**. One caravan returning shattered can condemn a road for a year. One triumphant column can make the same path fashionable and ruinously expensive within a week.

---

### **IV. Law at the Edge of Wilderness**

Emberweave stands with one foot in Crownweave and one in the wild. Its law is a compromise between the two.

The Weave Warden of Emberweave is usually a veteran of the road: sun-scarred, pragmatic, and far more interested in keeping blades sheathed than in filing neat reports. They understand that fear empties plazas faster than any tax.

The city watch here concentrates on:

* main caravan routes,  
* water depots,  
* the Ashward Plaza and major squares.

Between those points, the alleyways and back-lanes may belong—today—to whoever has the numbers or the nerve to hold them.

Fights in Emberweave are rarely subtle. They tend to be:

* fast,

* loud,

* and very public.

Yet there is an unspoken rule as firm as any written edict:

> **Do not spill enough blood to frighten the caravans.**

Those who break this compact discover that justice in Emberweave can arrive from several directions at once: the Watch, the Iron-Tusk lodges, or a sudden, very practical alliance of caravan masters who will not allow one fool to jeopardise an entire season’s livelihood.

### **V. Certain Places of Note**

#### **1\. The Ashward Plaza**

*Where journeys truly begin and end.*

At Emberweave’s centre lies the **Ashward Plaza**, a broad, sun-baked square of dark brick. At dawn and dusk it fills almost to bursting: wagons in crooked lines, animals tossing their heads, hirelings waiting with bedrolls over their shoulders and hope in their eyes.

Criers circle the edges, calling out routes and terms:

> “Three tendays to the Salt Reaches\! Hazard pay included—if you still have both legs at the end\!”

Scribes sit at low tables beneath patched awnings, ink-stained fingers racing as they write contracts in triplicate. At each corner of the plaza, a small shrine bears Crownweave’s thread motif; caravan masters often pause there to touch a charm or tie a length of coloured thread before they roll out.

Rumours of:

* new passes opened by storm,

* fresh camps of raiders,

* or strange sights at the far edge of the maps

tend to surface here first—usually shouted over cups, or muttered as someone tries to convince themselves one more guard is worth the coin.

---

#### **2\. The Brazen Rope Market**

*Everything a road-worn fool—or professional—might need.*

Just off Ashward Plaza, the alleys knot into the **Brazen Rope Market**. Its name comes from the heavy coils of rope hanging from iron hooks over the main lane, forming a rough archway that every traveller must duck beneath.

Within, a caravan can be outfitted from axle to tent-peg. Stalls sell:

* tent canvas and oiled tarps,

* nails, spikes, and spare wheel-bands,

* cured leather, packs, saddles, and harness,

* lamp oil, flint, torches, and hard rations.

Honest outfitters stand cheek by jowl with swindlers hawking rotted rope and watered oil. Emberweave’s philosophy applies: it is assumed that buyers know the difference—or will learn quickly.

Disputes over shoddy goods are often settled where they begin: sometimes in shouted curses, sometimes with fists, and occasionally with a brief, very calm visit from a captain who does not appreciate discovering that their gear fails a week from the nearest well.

---

#### **3\. The Embersteps**

*The last steps of the city, the first steps of the road.*

Cut into the outer slope of Crownweave’s southern wall, the **Embersteps** form a long, uneven flight of stone dropping from Emberweave’s lower streets to the gate below. Centuries of hooves, boots, and wheels have worn the treads into shallow hollows and chipped edges.

By old custom, a caravan is only considered **truly departed** once its last wagon has rattled beyond the final step. Families and friends line the upper landings to wave farewells. Many tie threads or ribbons to the railings, leaving them there until the caravan returns—or until the city’s caretakers cut them away at season’s end.

At twilight the Embersteps become a liminal space: neither fully city nor yet road. Smugglers, deal-makers, would-be heroes, and quiet fugitives brush past each other here, each pretending not to recognise the others’ reasons for leaving.

---

#### **4\. Iron-Tusk Lodges**

*Where steel, clan, and coin strike their own balance.*

Though the formal **Iron-Tusk Court** stands in the Sixfold Quarter, several of its lodges cluster close to Emberweave’s caravan routes. Long halls of timber and stone, banded in iron and hung with clan banners, mark where orcish interests and Crownweave’s roads intersect.

Inside, smoke-dark rafters hang above:

* trestle tables loaded with food and drink,

* weapon racks lined with scarred blades and spears,

* maps and trophy-shields nailed to the walls.

In the yards outside, warriors drill while clan envoys and caravan masters argue—sometimes in low voices, sometimes in full roar—over:

* the price of protection,

* what qualifies as “hazardous”,

* and how far a contract’s honour truly extends into the desert.

Away from the Court’s formal chambers, these lodges are where **steel and coin actually meet**. A single night’s talk in an Iron-Tusk hall can determine which caravan earns safe passage and which becomes a cautionary story told over ale.

---

#### **5\. The Dustveil Shrine**

*A way-altar for those who trust the Thread to guide their feet.*

Near the junction where Emberweave’s inner streets spill toward Ashward Plaza stands the **Dustveil Shrine**: a small, open-sided structure officially listed as a way-altar of the Temple of a Thousand Threads.

At its centre a low stone block is carved with an image of dust swirling and resolving into interwoven strands. The meaning is not subtle: every journey is another line added to the great pattern.

Caravan hands and wanderers pause to:

* burn a pinch of incense or travel herb,

* leave a coin or simple charm,

* tie a short length of coloured thread to belt, wagon, or weapon.

Prayers here are brief and unschooled. Some call to gods of roads, winds, or luck; others address the Thread itself, trusting that the pattern—whatever it is—will end with their return.

Clerics and acolytes visit Dustveil Shrine from time to time to clear old offerings and gather the sun-bleached threads. These are taken back to the Temple and burned in a quiet rite, a reminder that every road that begins in Emberweave still belongs to Crownweave’s greater tapestry.

---

#### **6\. The Embercoil Tavern**

*“Drink, plan, and tie your thread before you go.”*

A short walk from Ashward Plaza, at the meeting of three busy streets, stands the **Embercoil Tavern**—two storeys of red-tinged stone and a signboard painted with a simple spiral of flame. Many guidebooks do not list it. Caravans do.

Inside, the common room glows with warm light and smoke. The air smells of spiced stew and strong ale, the clatter of dice and tankards undercut by the low hum of negotiations in a half-dozen tongues. The ground floor is open hall and long tables; an upper loft ring overlooks the room, its back corridor lined with rented rooms and alcoves ideal for conspiracies and eavesdropping both.

A narrow side-passage known as **the Emberlane** serves as practice yard, duel ground, and meeting place for business not suited to the main street.

Behind the bar, **Mira “Three Roads” Calen**—a retired caravan master with scarred hands and a precise memory—keeps the house’s books and its temper. She is seconded by **Brahk of the Ashline**, an orc who treats security as another kind of road to patrol, and **Seli of the Coals**, a quick-tongued tiefling who somehow hears most rumours a day before they reach the Plaza.

Near the door, set into a small niche, is a thread-altar bearing the Crownweave sigil of a crown and three crossing strands. A bowl of coloured string rests beneath. Regulars have the habit of:

* taking a short piece,

* tying it to wrist, belt, or weapon hilt,

* and touching it briefly to their lips before stepping out into the night.

Some call it superstition, some courtesy to the Temple. In Embercoil, most simply say: **a little extra thread on your side never hurts.**

### **VI. The South Beyond the Walls**

*A gazetteer of the prefecture, arranged by region. Settlements of note are given in full; lesser ones in brief.*

Past the Emberward stormgate the land drops away from the Crownlands into warm river-country: half a dozen rivers run south to the sea through seasonal forest, savanna, and, nearer the coast, a thick green tangle that the Compendium’s older editions call jungle and Emberweavers call “the wet.” Only the west is truly dry. In the middle of it all stands the prefecture’s one mountain, and around its foot every road in the Weave eventually bends.

Locals divide the prefecture into five parts:

* **The Fjerfi Basin** – the river-country below the Crownlands, around the seat.
* **The Ashen Tableland** – the dry western upland, where the caravans that give the Weave its name actually live.
* **Emberpeak** – the lone mountain at the prefecture’s heart, and the mines and watch-posts on its slopes.
* **The Burning Coast** – the southern shore, facing the Iron Hordes.
* **The Saffron Shore** – the wet eastern coast and its spice-port.

Its principal routes are:

* the **Ember Road**, from The Crown through the Emberward stormgate to Fjerfi—the road the caravans of the city quarter actually take;
* the **Fjerfian Trail**, from Skyloom’s Central District south down the river valleys past Thornfold and Pepperwick to Fjerfi;
* the **Brand Road**, south from Fjerfi down the Newleighin valley, round the end of the ridge at Redbank, and along the shore to Brandmouth;
* the **Saffron Road**, east from Fjerfi past the foot of Emberpeak, through Slagbarrow and Tuskhold, to Saffron Quay;
* the **Downs Road**, north-east from Kilnbridge into Stormwake’s Lavenhurst Downs;
* and by water, the **Fjerfian Route**, the sea lane that enters the Newleighin at Lightermouth and runs up the river to Fjerfi’s quays.

Beyond these, the west is stitched together by caravan trails that move a little every season, and the coast by footpaths that move a little every storm.

#### **The Fjerfi Basin**

**Fjerfi** — *Seat of the Weave.*  
A walled river-port on the Newleighin, a day’s ride below the Emberward stormgate. Sea-going ships cannot reach it, but river-lighters can, and the Fjerfian Route brings cargo up from the coast to quays that are, by Emberweave standards, almost orderly. Fjerfi is where the Weave Warden holds court, where caravan charters for the south are sealed, and where the Iron-Tusk lodges of the city quarter keep their country halls. Its great plaza is a smaller, hotter twin of the Ashward; its temple keeps a Dustveil altar of its own. Anything that leaves the Crown for the south passes through Fjerfi, and is counted there.

**Pepperwick** and **Thornfold** — A village of pepper-gardens on the river below Fjerfi, and a thorn-hedged hamlet on the Skyloom side whose stock-pens hold the Weave’s spare draught animals.

**Gatewell** — *The Last Water.*  
A walled caravanserai just below the Emberward stormgate, built around a deep well and a long stone trough. Caravans bound for The Crown water their animals here, wash off the road, and put on whatever they intend to be seen arriving in.

**Spicegarth** — A village of walled spice-gardens on the slope south-east of Fjerfi, whose cumin and coriander season half the stews in the city quarter.

**Sunscar** — A village on the bare red ridge south of Fjerfi, named for the long scar of rock the Brand Road climbs. Its people sell water and shade to travellers on the ridge, at prices that make the climb feel longer.

#### **The Ashen Tableland**

**Cinderford** — *The Western Ford.*  
A plaza-town at the ford where Skyloom’s southern trails come down to Emberweave, and the first place in the Weave where the grass goes yellow. Cinderford is where caravans bound for the Tableland take on their last cheap water and their first expensive guides.

**Ashline** — *The Lodge in the Dust.*  
A walled caravanserai in the dry heart of the Tableland, held by the orc clans whose banners hang in the Iron-Tusk lodges of the city quarter. Every caravan that crosses the west stops at Ashline, pays the clans for passage, and is better guarded for it. The **Ashline Accords** take their name from it; the Compendium refers the reader to Volume XII for the circumstances, and declines to summarise them here.

**Glasspan** — *The Salt Flats.*  
A coastal village beside the white pans where the Tableland meets the sea. Its salt dries in crusts so clear they are sold as “glass salt” to the Crown’s better kitchens and the Collegium’s worse experiments. The **Glass Salt Uprisings** began in its salt-yards; see likewise Volume XII.

**Dustford** and **Tawnyford** — Two fords on the Tableland’s rivers: Dustford, where the coast trail from Skyloom crosses the Mitfordling, and Tawnyford, a herders’ hamlet in the tawny grass below Highdust.

**Highdust**, **Drywell**, and **Saltcliff** — The Tableland’s upland village, its one dependable well, and its southern fishing cove. Drywell’s water-keepers hold their office by inheritance and keep a ledger of every barrel drawn; there are older entries in it than in Fjerfi’s town charter.

#### **Emberpeak**

A single dark mountain rising out of the river-country, and the only true peak in the south. It is not a volcano, though the name and the colour of its rock persuade most visitors otherwise.

**Brandwatch** — *The Southern Eye.*  
A walled fort on Emberpeak’s summit. From its tower a watch-fire can be seen from Brandmouth to the Emberward stormgate, and on clear nights from the decks of ships halfway to the Iron Hordes. Brandwatch is held by Crown Guard veterans who were sent there, in their words, “to cool off.”

**Slagbarrow** — *The Mines.*  
A mining village on the mountain’s northern shoulder, where the Saffron Road crosses it. Its dwarven-run diggings bring up iron, copper, and a dark heavy ore that Northreach buys without saying why. The slag-heaps that name the village glow faintly after rain.

**Brandfoot** — A village at the western foot of the mountain, where the Perstoketon comes off its slopes. Brandfoot’s goats are the only creatures that climb Emberpeak for pleasure.

**Kilnbridge** — *The Wheelwrights’ Town.*  
A river-town north of Emberpeak where the Downs Road comes in from Stormwake. Its kilns, smithies, and wheelwright yards are the dwarven waystations of the city quarter writ large: half the axles and wheel-bands in the south were made or mended here.

#### **The Burning Coast**

**Brandmouth** — *The Southern Harbor.*  
A walled port at the mouth of the Kingham, and the Union’s nearest harbor to the Iron Hordes. Ships that dare the crossing load and unload here; so do the envoys, mercenaries, and soulstone-traders who make the crossing worth daring. Brandmouth’s plaza has a permanent market for orcish goods and a permanent watch for orcish trouble, and the two are often the same people on different days.

**Lightermouth** — *The River Door.*  
A plaza-town and port at the mouth of the Newleighin, where the Fjerfian Route leaves the sea for the river. Sea-going ships unload into the flat-bottomed lighters that give the town its name, and the lightermen’s guild, which owns every one of them, sets the price of everything that reaches Fjerfi by water.

**Perstoke** — *The Market Below the Mountain.*  
A plaza-town on the Perstoketon, in the warm valley under Emberpeak’s southern face. Slagbarrow’s ore comes down to it for smelting, Brandmouth’s imports come up to it for sale, and its weekly market is the busiest in the south outside Fjerfi.

**Redbank**, **Blackstrand**, and **Sunwick** — A river village where the Brand Road turns along the coast, a fishing village on the black-sand beach west of Brandmouth, and a cove-village at the prefecture’s south-western tip.

**Reedmouth**, **Charwick**, **Shellhythe**, and **Smokesands** — Fishing villages along the Burning Coast; Shellhythe’s divers bring up the pearl-shell that Saffron Quay sells as Emberfall work. Charwick and Smokesands cure their catch in smokehouses that stain the whole shore grey; Reedmouth, on the next river east of Brandmouth, takes the overflow when Brandmouth’s harbor is full.

#### **The Saffron Shore**

**Saffron Quay** — *The Spice-Port.*  
A plaza-port at the mouth of the Knavereley, the Weave’s door to the eastern sea and to Emberfall’s trade. Spice, dye, resin, and saffron come ashore here, and most of the spice stalls in the city quarter are stocked from its warehouses. Its temple is the only one on the coast with a Dustveil altar *and* a sea-shrine, and keeps both busy.

**Tuskhold** — *The Clan Town.*  
A walled river-town on the Saffron Road, built around an Iron-Tusk lodge large enough to be called a fortress. Caravans heading east hire their guards here; caravans arriving from the east report to it, whether or not they meant to.

**Dyewater** and **Oxbow** — Two river villages on the Knavereley: Dyewater, whose vats turn the river below it red, blue, or saffron depending on the day, and Oxbow, a ferry-and-cattle village on the great bend upstream.

**Palmhaven** and **Vinehollow** — A fishing village in the palms south of Saffron Quay, and a hamlet of vine-growers in the wet woods inland.

**Tinderholt** and **Cinnabar Point** — A hamlet of charcoal-burners in the wet woods below Emberpeak, and a fishing village north of Saffron Quay whose red cliffs are mined for vermilion pigment.

### **VII. Advice to the Traveller**

*A marginal note, copied from a dog-eared guide owned by a spice-merchant of the Ashward:*

* “If someone offers you rope at half the going rate, buy two lengths—one to test, and one to hang them with if it snaps.”

* “Never argue prices on the Embersteps. If you’ve reached the stairs, you’ve already agreed to go.”

* “When the Iron-Tusk lodges are quiet, listen harder. Loud orcs are full; silent orcs are thinking.”

* “In Embercoil, do not boast about the roads you have walked. Someone at the next table has walked them farther, hungrier, and with worse company.”

*Thus ends the entry on Emberweave. For the matter of its caravans’ involvement in the Glass Salt Uprisings and the Ashline Accords, see Volume XII: “On Wars That Arrive by Wagon.”*
