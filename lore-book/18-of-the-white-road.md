# Of The White Road

*Excerpt from **The Crownweave Compendium**, Fourth Roadwarden’s Edition*  
 *Shelfmark: TV-SB-001, “On the Road to the Canopy Court”*

---

### **Of Sakbeleth, the White Road**

A traveller from Crownweave reaches Thal’vireth after thirty-odd days at sea, and the first thing they see of it is not the forest, nor the Tower, nor the tree grown through it. It is a line.

It begins at the quays of **Nabireth**, at the head of the Quiet Gulf, and it runs west into the trees as straight as a rule laid on a chart: a raised causeway of pale, close-fitted stone, broad enough for two carts to pass, lifted a little above the forest floor on a bank of packed earth. The elves call it *sakbe*, which in the old tongue means nothing grander than “white road.” It runs for some three hundred kilometres, ten days’ walk at a steady pace, and at its far end it climbs into the lower boughs of Ix’kaanil to the steps of the **Canopy Court**.

The district that keeps it is **Sakbeleth**, and Sakbeleth is very little more than the Road and what has been built beside it: a harbour-town at one end, a stair at the other, and between them a string of inns, toll-posts, exchange halls and villages, none of them more than a few hours’ walk from the stone. It is the narrowest district in the realm and by far the busiest. It is also the only one that keeps true time.

If the Green Cradle feeds Thal’vireth and the Deep Count remembers it, **Sakbeleth is where it lets the rest of the world in: a little way, at a walking pace, and on its own terms.**

---

### **I. The Temper of the Road**

Sakbeleth is a district of **straight lines, slow queues, and true hours**.

Everything else in Thal’vireth bends. Roads follow the ridges and the riverbanks; villages sit wherever the ground and the tree allow; the districts themselves run fast or slow according to how the tree spends its memory on them. The White Road alone does not bend, does not hurry and does not wait. Its stones are cut from the same pale rock as the Verdant Tower and set with wards that hold the ground beneath them at **outside time**, the hour kept at sea and in Crownweave. A day on the Road is a day. A year on the Road is a year. For a realm built on the difference between the two, this makes the Road the one place where nobody has to do arithmetic.

Travellers who have crossed a Count-gate describe stepping back onto the Road as **breathing out**. The air feels ordinary. Sounds arrive when they are made. A candle burns for the length of a candle. Elves of the faster and slower districts come down to the Road for exactly this reason, to rest from their own pace for a day, and the Road’s inns keep rooms for them as well as for foreigners.

Along its length stand **the Ten Stones**, one at the end of each day’s walk from the sea, the first a day out of Nabireth and the tenth at the foot of the Canopy Steps, each a squared pillar of the Road’s own pale rock cut with the day’s number and the true hour at sunrise. Couriers reckon by them, carters are paid by them, and every inn on the Road is said to lie “past the third” or “short of the seventh.”

Once a year the Road stops. At the year’s end the old calendar keeps five nameless days, the *wayeb*, when nothing is begun. Through those five days the Daykeepers walk the Road from the sea to the Steps, stone by stone, renewing the wards that keep it true. Nothing moves on it while they work. Ships that arrive in the Gulf during the wayeb ride at anchor off Nabireth until it is over, and their crews call the five days “the Long Tide,” for reasons that have more to do with the harbour taverns than the sea.

---

### **II. Those Who Walk It**

Sakbeleth gathers those whose living is made **between Thal’vireth and everyone else**.

Elves are the largest part of its people, as everywhere in the realm, but the Road’s elves are a particular kind: traders, carters, innkeepers, translators, toll-keepers and the staff of the re-acclimation houses, who have chosen to live their whole lives at true time and are faintly looked down on for it elsewhere. A Road-elf’s cord of Count-beads is long and almost entirely white. In the fast districts this is thought dull. In the slow ones it is thought hasty. On the Road it is thought sensible, and Road-elves will say so at length.

Humans are the largest share of its foreigners and the only foreigners who stay in numbers: Crownweave factors, sailors who missed their ship home, mages licensed to study under the Ah K’in and waiting on the Court for permission to go further, and a few families who came three generations ago and never quite left. Most live in Nabireth, rarely deeper.

Half-elves are common along the Road, more so than anywhere else in the realm. Many are the children of the trade itself, and the Road is the one district where their uneasy standing, legally elven and culturally caught between fast and slow, matters least.

Tieflings work the Exchange Houses as brokers and contract-scribes, as they do in Emberweave and for the same reason: they are very good at it, and the elves would rather not be.

Dwarves are fewer, and almost all are smiths on contract to the Balam Order, travelling up the Road to the Jaguar Reach with their tools and coming back down it with their pay.

Then there are the **clients**: the foreigners who come to Thal’vireth not to trade with it but to buy time from it. Some come with cargo to be aged or kept. Some come for themselves. The Road knows them by sight. They travel slowly, they ask the price of everything in lived years, and they are always, in some way, in a hurry. The saddest of them are carried. The Waiting Wards of the Deep Count take the dying of the outside world and keep them at a day or so per year until a cure is found, and the Road is how they get there: in curtained litters, on the shoulders of hired bearers, with a physician walking beside and a lawyer walking behind. Road-elves call this **the slow procession**, and step off the stone to let it pass.

### **III. Of Coin, Cargo, and Contract**

Sakbeleth is **Thal’vireth’s one door**, and everything that passes through a door is counted.

Coming down the Road to the sea:

* grain, beans, cacao and honey from the Green Cradle, harvested four and five times a year;
* glyph-lamps, root-grown tools and instruments from the Quickening Wards;
* tinctures and medicines aged to strength in weeks;
* jade-wood, bark-cloth and quetzal-dyed textiles;
* and, rarely and only with the Canopy Court’s assent, a few grams of Verdant soulstone dust.

Coming up the Road from the sea:

* Crownsblood, and the goods it buys: iron, steel and tools, salt, wool, glass and wine;
* Crownweave books, which the Hollow Archive buys and does not lend back;
* cargo consigned for **chronoflow**;
* and the clients who paid for it.

Within the Road, trade is reckoned in **jade-tally**: thin jade tokens cut in five sizes and stamped by the Daykeepers. Foreign coin is changed for it at the Exchange Houses of Koneleth, and only there; tally changed anywhere else is a crime, and tally changed at Koneleth costs a tenth part to the house that changes it. Crownweave factors complain about the tenth constantly and pay it every time.

The Road’s real business, and the Curia’s real income, is **chronoflow**: the sale of other districts’ time to people who do not live in them. Every chronoflow contract is written at Koneleth, witnessed by a Daykeeper, and priced by the **lived year**, which is to say by how much of a district’s time it will use. They come in three kinds:

* **Ageing** sends cargo into a fast district to grow older quickly: wine and brandy laid down for a decade and brought back in two years; timber seasoned; cheese ripened; tinctures and cures brought to strength. The Green Cradle and the Quickening Wards both take ageing contracts, and both resent how little of the price reaches them.
* **Keeping** sends cargo into a slow district to stay as it is: seed, perishable medicines, documents a client would rather not see age, and once, by a contract still on file at Koneleth, an entire wedding cake.
* **Waiting** is keeping, for people. It is sold only for the Waiting Wards, only to the dying, and only with the Canopy Court’s assent, and it is the most expensive thing in Thal’vireth.

Larger contracts, and any that touch soulstone, must go up the Road to the Canopy Court for assent. Petitioners say the Court is slow. The Court, which sits in the slowest part of the realm, has not noticed.

---

### **IV. Law on the Road**

Sakbeleth is held to the realm’s law more strictly than any other district, because it is the only one foreigners see.

Its **Ah K’in**, the Keeper of the Count for the Road, holds the bond that lets them read and set the Road’s time, and keeps it true. The present Ah K’in of the Road is said to be capable, patient and very well informed about the Exchange Houses; the Compendium has been asked not to print their name, and has agreed. Below them, the Road’s **Daykeepers** run its customs, keep its Ledgers, stamp its tally and witness its contracts. Its peace is kept by a standing detachment of the **Balam Order**, who patrol the stone from the Landing to the Steps and take no orders from the Ah K’in that the Order’s war-captain has not seen first.

On the Road:

* **Weapons** may be worn openly. They may not be drawn on the stone itself except by the Balam.
* **Chronoflow magic** worked anywhere outside a sanctioned rite is treated as an act of war against the district it is worked in. On the Road, this includes any attempt to tamper with the wards in the stone. The Balam are not asked to arrest for it.
* **Soulstone** may not be bought, sold or carried anywhere on the Road without a Canopy Court seal. A foreigner found with it is put back on the first ship out. A Road-elf found with it is sent to the Broken Deep.
* **Ledgers** must be stamped at every Count-gate. A foreigner’s Ledger is opened at the Counting Pier on landing and closed there on leaving, and the Daykeepers will not let a ship sail until it is.

Customs at Nabireth are thorough, slow, and, in the Compendium’s experience, honest about almost everything. Cargo is opened, weighed, counted and entered. The one general exception, written into the Road’s customs-code since before the Compact, is cargo **consigned by name to an Ah K’in**, which passes to its consignee unopened as a courtesy to the Keepers of the Count. The Compendium notes the custom without comment. The Daykeepers at Nabireth know which crates not to open, and are not paid to wonder why.

### **V. Certain Places of Note**

#### **1\. The Counting Pier**

*Where a foreigner’s years begin to be counted.*

Every ship that docks at Nabireth ties up first at the **Counting Pier**, a long jetty of jade-wood at the centre of the quays, and every passenger walks its length before setting foot on the Road. At its landward end stands a low, open hall where the Daykeepers of the Landing sit at a row of tables under a carved frieze of the Ten Stones.

Here each foreigner is asked their name, their purpose, and their age, and a **Ledger of Hours** is opened in their name: a slim book of bark-paper in which the Daykeepers will record every Count-gate they cross and every lived year they spend in the realm. It is handed to them sealed with the Road’s white stamp. The same entry goes up the Road the next morning, with the Pier’s other copies, to be written into the great register at Tun’iel. Visitors who lose it may have it reopened, at the Pier, at a cost of one day’s wait and a fine. Visitors who lose it beyond the Road are advised to come back to the Pier as fast as they can, and to bring witnesses.

On leaving, the Ledger is closed at the same tables and kept in the realm. Thal’vireth does not let foreigners take their lived years home with them. The Daykeepers say this is a matter of record. Foreigners who have spent a decade in the Deep Count and come out a year older do not always find this comforting.

---

#### **2\. The Exchange Houses of Koneleth**

*Where outside coin becomes inside time.*

Halfway up the Road, on the fifth day out of Nabireth, the forest opens around a broad, paved square, and on three sides of it stand the **Exchange Houses**: long halls of grown wood on pale stone footings, each the seat of a trading house licensed by the Curia. Their doors stand open from the first true hour to the last, and their floors are crowded.

At the front of each hall, brokers change Crownsblood and foreign coin for jade-tally at the Curia’s rate, less the tenth. At the back, behind carved screens, the **contract-rooms** write chronoflow: a Daykeeper at every table, a broker on one side and a client on the other, and between them a ledger of rates by district and by lived year that changes, very slightly, every season. The largest Crownweave trading houses keep permanent factors in Koneleth, with rooms of their own behind the screens, and the elves of the Road call these rooms, without much affection, **the cartel benches**.

The busiest broker in Koneleth is a tiefling who goes by **Candour**, as the tieflings of Emberfall go by the virtue they mean to be known for, and who has worked the same table in the same hall for twenty-two outside years and will tell any client, unprompted, what each of their contracts will cost them in lived years, in jade-tally, and in “what the Cradle will say about it afterwards.” Candour keeps no permanent loyalties and many permanent accounts. A good deal of what the cartel benches know, they learned from Candour’s table, and a good deal of what the Curia knows about the cartel benches came the same way.

---

#### **3\. The Quiet Rooms of Weneleth**

*Where the Count-sick come to be ordinary again.*

On the seventh day out of Nabireth, a few days below the Canopy Steps, the Road passes through **Weneleth**, a town of inns. Its name means, more or less, “sleeping place,” and it means it. Weneleth’s houses sell rest at true time: dim rooms, thick walls, plain food, no bells, and a Daykeeper on call in every house to correct a guest’s Ledger and stamp the hours they have slept.

These are the **re-acclimation houses**, and a night in one is the accepted cure for Count-sickness, the nausea, lost words and missing afternoons that come of crossing between a fast district and a slow one in too short a time. Travellers coming down from the Deep Count stop at Weneleth before they go any further. Travellers going up to it stop there first, to rest before they need to. The good houses are booked out for weeks in season, and the best, **the House of Even Hours**, takes bookings only by letter from Crownweave, a year ahead, with a deposit.

Weneleth is also where the slow procession rests on its way up the Road to the Waiting Wards. Most of the inns keep a curtained room on the ground floor for litters. Nobody in Weneleth asks who is in them.

---

#### **4\. The Ten Stones**

*How the Road counts itself.*

The Ten Stones stand at the end of each day’s walk from the sea, the first a day out of Nabireth, the tenth at the foot of the Canopy Steps. Each is a squared pillar about twice a tall man’s height, cut on its seaward face with its number and on its landward face with the true hour at that morning’s sunrise, recut by the Daykeepers every wayeb. The stones carry the strongest of the Road’s wards, and the stretch of stone around each is the truest ground in the realm.

By custom, a contract witnessed at the foot of one of the Ten Stones cannot later be disputed on the grounds that either party had lost track of the time. Brokers who expect trouble from a client sometimes walk them out to the nearest Stone to sign.

---

#### **5\. The Strands**

*Why the Road looks frayed from above.*

From the drum-tower at Lak’inir, on the forest’s edge north of Nabireth, the White Road looks less like a road than like **a single pale hair that has been brushed too often**. For its first few days out of the Tower it runs alone. Then, from about the seventh Stone and the inns of Weneleth onward, foot-trails begin to peel away from it, leaving the stone at a shallow angle, running beside it for a while, then splitting, and splitting again, toward the farming villages of the Green Cradle on either side. The nearer the Road comes to the sea, the more it frays.

The elves call these trails **the Strands**. Each leaves the Road through a small arch of grown wood, a Count-gate with a Daykeeper’s post beside it, because a step off the stone is a step out of true time and into the Cradle’s, where a season passes in weeks. Strand-gates are the busiest Count-gates in the realm. Cradle farmers bring their harvest down the Strands to the Road and carry back salt, iron and gossip, and a strand-gate Daykeeper may stamp a hundred Ledgers on a market morning.

Foreigners are advised that the Strands are not part of Sakbeleth, that the Cradle beyond each gate is fast, and that a day spent walking out to see the terraces may cost them a week of their life. The Strands themselves, and the villages at their ends, are described with the Green Cradle.

---

#### **6\. The Canopy Steps**

*The end of the Road, and the beginning of the wait.*

At its inland end the White Road reaches the roots of Ix’kaanil and turns upward. The **Canopy Steps** are a broad stair, half cut stone and half living root, climbing into the lowest boughs of the tree, and at their top stands the **Canopy Court**, where the Ah K’in receive petitioners and grant or refuse assent.

The Steps are where the Road’s true time ends. The Court above them lies in the Deep Count, and keeps its pace. Petitioners queue on the Steps for days, sometimes weeks, with their papers in oilcloth and their Ledgers in their hands, in a line that the Balam keep orderly and the Road’s pedlars keep fed. A petitioner who is called up to the Court at sunset may come back down the Steps at noon the next day and find that, at the bottom, a week has passed.

The tenth Stone stands at the foot of the Steps. Petitioners who have waited long enough have carved their names and the dates of their arrival into its lower course, against every regulation, for longer than the Compendium has existed. The Daykeepers recut the hour on its face every wayeb and have never once touched the names.

---

#### **7\. The Long Tide**

*“Drink your days slowly. You only get the one kind here.”*

On the Nabireth waterfront, three doors down from the Counting Pier, stands the **Long Tide**, a two-storey inn of grown wood with a ship’s figurehead over its door and a painted signboard of the harbour at anchor. It is the house where Crownweave sailors drink, where foreign factors meet their contacts, and where any traveller who wants to know what is happening on the Road should go first.

The ground floor is a long common room with the bar at one end and a great window over the Gulf at the other; the upper floor is rooms, and a gallery from which it is possible to hear most of what is said below. It takes its name, and much of its trade, from the wayeb, when every ship in the Gulf is waiting and every sailor in Nabireth is in its common room.

The innkeeper, **Hester Crane**, is a human, a Crownweave mariner who came ashore at Nabireth for one night twenty-six years ago, missed the tide, and decided that she liked a town where the hours stayed put. She keeps outside time, a short temper and a long memory for ships: which came in, what they carried, who met them, and which of them were met by somebody the Daykeepers did not ask questions of. She does not sell this information. She does, occasionally, give it away.

### **VI. From the Sea to the Steps**

*A gazetteer of the district, from the Landing inland. Settlements of note are given in full; lesser ones in brief. Distances are in days’ walk from the Counting Pier.*

The Road divides, by the habit of those who walk it, into four stretches:

* **The Landing** – Nabireth and the harbour villages about it, where the Road begins and the Strands are thickest.
* **The Lower Road** – the second to fourth days, through the Green Cradle’s busiest country.
* **The Exchange** – Koneleth and the square at the Road’s midpoint.
* **The Upper Road** – the sixth day to the tenth, quieter, older, and closer to the Tower.

The **Granary Road**, which carries the Cradle’s harvest from the Threshing Mounds at Chaakvael down to the sea, joins the White Road for its last stretch into Nabireth, and the **Root-Road**, which circles the Tower and links the districts, crosses it at Weneleth. The Strands are described with the Green Cradle.

#### **The Landing**

**Nabireth** — *The Landing.*  
The realm’s only legal harbour, built on piers over the shallows at the head of the Quiet Gulf, and its largest town by the outside count, at about eighteen thousand. Every ship from the east makes landfall here, every foreigner is entered at the Counting Pier, and every cargo is opened, weighed and counted on its quays, with one exception. Nabireth keeps true time and a waterfront of inns, warehouses and chandlers that would not look out of place in Stormwake; behind it the town climbs a low rise to the gate-tower where the Road begins. The Long Tide is on its waterfront, the Daykeepers of the Landing in its customs-hall, and a standing detachment of the Balam in its gate-tower.

**Ja’eleth** — *The Water-Road.*  
A village on the shore just south of Nabireth, beside the stream that gives the harbour its fresh water. Its people fill and sell ships’ water by the barrel, ferry passengers out to vessels at anchor during the wayeb, and keep the only boat-yard in the realm that will repair a foreign hull.

**Najeleth** and **Ki’eleth** — Two villages of the Landing a day’s walk out: Najeleth, *the house-road*, at the head of a Strand north of the Road, where Nabireth’s carters and porters keep their families; and Ki’eleth, *the sweet road*, a hamlet on the Granary Road south of it, where the Cradle’s fruit and honey wait in cool sheds for a ship.

#### **The Lower Road**

**Kaajeleth** — *The Town-Road.*  
A village on the stone itself at the second Stone, with a market-square on either side of the Road, where Strands from both halves of the Cradle come in. On market mornings Kaajeleth’s strand-gates are the busiest in the realm.

**Ookeleth** — *The Foot-Road.*  
A village south of the Road where the Granary Road and three Strands meet, and the last place on the way to the sea where a carter can change oxen. Its name is a joke at the expense of everyone who arrives in it on foot after the oxen have all been hired.

#### **The Exchange**

**Koneleth** — *The Exchange Houses.*  
A town of about seven thousand at the Road’s midpoint, built around the square of the Exchange Houses, where foreign coin is changed for jade-tally and chronoflow contracts are written and sealed. The cartel benches, the contract-rooms and Candour’s table are all here, and so are most of the Road’s lawyers. The fifth Stone stands in the middle of its square.

#### **The Upper Road**

**Chi’eleth** — *The Road’s Mouth.*  
A village on the stone at the sixth Stone, below Weneleth, where the Strands begin to leave the Road in earnest. Its strand-gate is the oldest on the Road, and the village is named for it.

**Weneleth** — *The Sleeping Place.*  
A town of inns a few days below the Canopy Steps, where the Root-Road crosses the White Road. Its re-acclimation houses sell rest at true time and are the accepted cure for Count-sickness; its ground-floor rooms receive the slow procession on its way to the Waiting Wards.

**Ts’uleleth** — *The Strangers’ Road.*  
A hamlet on a stream a day and a half short of the Canopy Steps, where foreigners waiting on the Court lodge when the Steps are too crowded to sleep on. Its houses are let by the week and its one shrine is kept, by long custom, for gods the realm does not have.

### **VII. Advice to the Traveller**

*A marginal note, copied from a guide kept at the Long Tide for the use of Crownweave factors:*

* “Keep your Ledger on your person, dry, and stamped. Nobody in Thal’vireth will believe you are as old as you say without it, and some of them will profit from not believing you.”

* “Change your coin at Koneleth and nowhere else. The tenth is robbery. Everything else is worse.”

* “Never step off the stone without meaning to. The Strands are pretty and the Cradle is lovely and you will come back a fortnight older for one afternoon’s walk.”

* “If the Road closes for the wayeb while you are on it, stop where you are, find an inn and stay in it. The Daykeepers do not like being watched at work, and the Balam like it less.”

* “When the slow procession passes, step off the road to the left and do not look into the litters. It is not forbidden. It is simply never done.”

* “If a crate comes off a ship at Nabireth unopened, it was not your crate, and you did not see it.”

*Thus ends the entry on Sakbeleth and the White Road. For the Green Cradle, whose villages the Road’s Strands reach, and for the Canopy Court at its inland end, see the entries that follow.*
