# 👑 Crownweave

# **Crownweave**

*City of a Hundred Crowns, Loom of the Realms*

No crown stands alone. In **Crownweave**, that saying is not a proverb—it’s law, history, and threat.

Crownweave is two things at once. It is a **city**—The Crown and its walled Weaves, perched on a broad hill where roads and river-trade knot together above the Meridian Spire. And it is a **Union**: the whole island on which that city stands, set in the middle sea at the centre of Tessarion, with the five Tower-nations ranged around it across the water. When a Crownweaver says “the city,” they mean the walls. When they say “Crownweave,” they usually mean everything.

Either way, it serves as the de facto capital of humankind in Tessarion. Here, banners from half a hundred realms hang over the same streets, and the clatter of markets never truly sleeps.

**Population:**

* **The city:** \~80,000 souls within the walls (swelling to well over 120,000 in the height of the trade season)
* **The Union:** several hundred thousand more across the island’s prefectures—more people in The Iron Crucible and its highlands alone, some say, than in the whole of The Crown, though The Crown’s census-takers dispute this annually.

The figures that follow describe the city unless otherwise noted.

* **Humans (\~55%)**  
   The beating heart of Crownweave. Humans fill the city’s markets, barracks, counting-houses and council chambers. Most merchants, stewards, petty nobles and street rabble share one thing in common: mortal, short-lived, and utterly convinced the city belongs to *them*.

* **Elves (\~12%)**  
   Graceful and distant, the elves of Crownweave are most often seen as diplomats, sages, and mages. Many keep refined estates in Skyloom and the quieter Weaves, where gardens and libraries rise above the noise of the streets.

* **Dwarves (\~10%)**  
  Stocky, stubborn, and dependable, dwarves cluster near forges, shipyards, and stoneworks. They are smiths, masons, bankers and foremen—those who turn metal, stone and coin into things that last longer than any treaty.

* **Orcs (\~8%)**  
   Orcs most often serve as caravan guards, mercenaries, city watch enforcers, dock-hands, and bodyguards. Behind the muscle, however, stand shrewd clan envoys who play the game of politics with as much cunning as any courtier.

* **Tieflings (\~8%)**  
   Marked by infernal blood yet sharpened by city life, tieflings tend to gravitate toward spheres of information, magic, and grey-market commerce. Scribes, pact-brokers, spellwrights, whisper merchants and spies—if there is a secret to be sold, a tiefling likely knows the price.

* **Sangrith (\~7%)**  
   The rarest and most unsettling of the six. Sangrith are closely associated with blood-rites, oaths bound in flesh, and magic that makes even hardened mercenaries step aside. Their presence in Crownweave is small, but every rumor involving them is told in a lower voice.

## **Origins – The Weaving of Crowns**

Generations ago, the lands of men were torn apart by the **War of Split Banners**. No lord could claim a clear victory; winter famines and empty coffers did what swords could not.

The island had an older name then—**Hexaspire**, after the six Towers that can be felt, if not seen, from its central basin—and it was held to be poor, unsettled country, good for little but the crossing. On a modest hill-town by a deep river crossing at the basin’s heart, directly above the Meridian Spire, the warring rulers gathered for one last parley. Before witness and steel, they swore the **Weaving Oath**:

*“Let no crown stand alone. May our strength be woven, not broken.”*

The town was renamed **Crownweave**, and its hilltop hall declared neutral ground. Since that day, each human realm maintains a seat there—some honored, some merely tolerated, none forgotten.

The old name did not die. Storytellers, priests, and scholars of the Towers still speak of **Hexaspire** when they mean the basin, the Spire beneath it, or the island as it was before the Oath. In the taverns of the lower Weaves, “Hexaspire” is what you call the place when you want to sound as though you remember something the Council does not.

## **The Crown**

At the city’s heart rises **The Crown**, a ring of ancient stone walls and steep streets that cling to the central hill.

* **Hall of the Woven Crown**  
   A great chamber of carved pillars and inlaid floors where the **Council of Crowns** convenes. Seats wait for each sovereign or their envoy; a few remain empty, their realms lost, fallen… or whispered to endure in shadow.  
* **The Old Loom**  
   Labyrinthine offices, archive-vaults, and counting houses where scribes, advocates, and clerks spin law and coin into power. It is said that more wars have been averted—or quietly arranged—within these walls than on any battlefield.
* **The Spire Corridors**
   Beneath The Crown, sealed transit halls lead down toward the approved observation levels of the Meridian Spire. Most citizens will never walk them. All of them live on top of them.

### **The Crownlands and the Everstorm**

Around the walls lie the **Crownlands**—marked on Loom Council charts, with bureaucratic flatness, as the prefecture of *Central City*. It is farmland, orchard, and estate, without a single chartered town: by old law, nothing within the Crownlands may be chartered but The Crown itself.

Over the Crownlands hangs the **Everstorm**: a slow, silent ring of churning cloud that encircles the heart of the island and has never, in recorded memory, dispersed. It brings no lightning and little rain. It simply turns. The Loomwardens of Skyloom keep it from drifting; scholars of the Collegium hold that it marks the line at which the Meridian Spire’s mediation of the Outer Towers’ pressures is strongest, though every such paper is careful to say *appears to mark*. The great roads pass beneath it through the **stormgates**, and travellers arriving from the Weaves speak of the moment the light changes overhead as the moment they truly arrived.

### **A Capital of Record**

By an old quirk of maritime law, foreign charters and ships’ papers name **Caer Veyn**, the harbor city of Stormwake, as the Union’s seat rather than The Crown. The Crown finds this convenient. It means that foreign disputes arrive in Caer Veyn first, and are frequently settled there before anyone in the Hall of the Woven Crown has to hear of them.

## **The Weaves**

Beyond the old walls, the city spreads in concentric rings of districts called **Weaves**. Each Weave began as a gate-quarter facing a distant land, its people, and its trade flowing into the city.

As the Union grew, each Weave’s name stretched outward along its roads until it covered not just the quarter within the walls but a whole **prefecture** of the island beyond them. Today a Weave is both: a crowded ring of streets inside the city, and a province of towns, forts, and harbors outside it, governed from its own seat and answering to the Loom Council through its Warden.

* **Skyloom** *(the west)* – Within the walls, slim towers and observatories at the foot of the Skyward Towers. Beyond them, the green western hill-country beneath the floating Loom, facing the sea toward Thal’Vireth, governed from **Central District**. Arcanists, star-readers, Loomwardens, and winged folk. *See: Of Skyloom, the Loom Above.*
* **Northreach Weave** *(the north)* – Within the walls, stone longhouses, smoke-stained beams, and forges ringing day and night. Beyond them, the cold northern highlands facing Khazrund and the Obsidian Crown across the water, governed from **The Iron Crucible**. Iron, artifice, Hearthmark coin, and hardy dwarven clans. *See: Of Northreach Weave, the Iron Threshold.*
* **Stormwake Weave** *(the east)* – Within the walls, salty air, shipping offices, and shrines to sea-gods. Beyond them, the great eastern bay of the Wakewater and the peninsula called the Hook, governed from the harbor city of **Caer Veyn**. Captains, factors, and every foreign coin in Tessarion. *See: Of Stormwake, the Open Harbor of the East.*
* **Emberweave** *(the south)* – Within the walls, canvas awnings in bright colors shading spice stalls and hookah dens. Beyond them, the broad, dry, thinly settled south, facing the Iron Hordes across the water. Traders from hot deserts and sun-baked plains bargain in a dozen tongues. *See: Of Emberweave, the Burning Gate of the South.*
* **Underweave** *(beneath)* – The one Weave with no land beyond the walls. The city’s underside: vaulted under-bridges, half-flooded alleys, and forgotten arches. Thieves’ guilds, smugglers, and those who “slip between the threads” make their lives in the dim light. It has no prefecture, no seat, and—officially—no Warden worth the name.

At the centre of them all lie the Crownlands, which belong to no Weave and to The Crown alone.

As new alliances are forged, new Weaves are raised. When ties are broken, a Weave can wither into ruin—or be claimed by darker powers.

## **Rule and Intrigue**

### **Layers of Power**

#### **The Council of Crowns**

At the highest, most ceremonial level stands the **Council of Crowns**—a convocation of human rulers and their envoys.

* **Seat.** The **Hall of the Woven Crown**, in The Crown district.  
* **Function.**  
  * Ratifies grand treaties between human realms.  
  * Declares formal war and peace.  
  * Endorses or denounces major trade pacts affecting all mankind.

* **Reality.**  
  The Council gathers infrequently, often only in crisis or on commemorative dates. Many chairs sit empty or are represented by “acting regents” whose legitimacy is in question. When the Council *does* act with unity, kingdoms fall or rise in a single decree.

> *“When the Council speaks with one voice, every other tongue in Tessarion falls silent.”*  
>  — old Crownweave saying

#### **The Loom Council**

Beneath the crowns lies the **Loom Council**, which actually keeps the city running.

* **Composition.**

  * The **Lord Steward of Crownweave** (nominal head of city government).  
  * Wardens from major Weaves.  
  * Representatives from the most powerful guilds.  
  * A small number of sanctioned religious and arcane advisors (including, occasionally, a Threadkeeper).

* **Responsibilities.**

  * Setting tariffs, customs duties, and docking fees.  
  * Maintaining the city watch and garrison deployments.  
  * Granting or revoking market rights, guild charters, and large building permits.  
  * Interpreting the Charter of the Six within city law.

Day to day, the Loom Council’s decisions matter far more to common folk than the Council of Crowns. A shift of one percent in caravan tolls can starve a Weave more surely than a lost battle.

#### **The Charter and the Six**

Crownweave is bound not only by human oaths, but by the **Charter of the Six**—the foundational treaty that recognizes six major peoples as co-signatories in the city’s creation:

* Humans  
* Elves  
* Dwarves  
* Orcs  
* Tieflings  
* Sangrith

The Charter forbids racial segregation by law and guarantees each people:

* The right to dwell, work, and worship in any Weave.  
* The right to maintain an embassy in the city.  
* The right to appeal directly to the Loom Council when the Charter is threatened.

### **Law and Order**

Crownweave has little patience for chaos that endangers trade, but considerable tolerance for quiet corruption.

#### **The Crown Guard**

Elite soldiers sworn to defend The Crown, the Hall of the Woven Crown, and high dignitaries.

* **Role.**  
  * Protect key sites and persons.  
  * Enforce high edicts of the Council of Crowns and Loom Council.

* **Reputation.**  
   Professional, disciplined, and grim. Bribery is rare, but not extinguished.

#### **Weave Wardens & City Watch**

Each major Weave is assigned a **Warden**, overseeing a mixed force of watchmen in both its city quarter and its prefecture. Wardens hold court at their Weave’s seat—Central District, The Iron Crucible, Caer Veyn—and keep a deputy within the walls.

* **Variation.**
   Law in Northreach is stern and methodical; in Skyloom, quiet and precise; in Stormwake, light-handed and selective; in Emberweave, noisy and flexible; in Underweave, selective at best.    
* **Practice.**  
   Public violence is suppressed quickly, especially near trade arteries. Crime that learns to keep quiet—and pay its share—often survives.

#### **Law in Play**

* **Open weapons.** Common and tolerated, though heavy war gear is restricted near The Crown.  
* **Magic.** Non-destructive magic is usually ignored; obvious battle spells draw swift response.  
* **Diplomatic Ground.** Within the Sixfold Quarter, guards tread carefully: a misstep can become an international incident.

### **Factions and Hidden Hands**

Several factions pull on Crownweave’s threads from behind curtains and counting tables.

#### **Merchants’ Loom**

A loose but powerful consortium of the city’s wealthiest trade houses.

* **Goal.** Keep Crownweave as the pricing center for grain, ore, and countless goods across Tessarion.  
* **Tactics.** Coordinated price setting, embargo threats, funding “independent” mercenary actions, and quietly backing Loom Council candidates.

#### **Threadkeepers**

A mystic order that claims to read fate itself as a tapestry of threads.

* **Role.**

  * Advisers to those who can afford them—or those they choose to approach.  
  * Keepers of omen-laden prophecies and dangerous histories.

* **Belief.**  
   Nothing is truly random; individuals are threads woven through a design glimpsed only in fragments.

It is considered both a blessing and a curse to have one’s “thread” noticed by the order.

#### **The Silent Shuttle**

A clandestine network that trades in information, blackmail, and “accidents.”

* **Public Face.** None. The name is seldom spoken aloud.  
* **Activity.**  
  * Smuggling and espionage.  
  * Arranging disappearances—of documents, scandals, or people.

* **Relationship to the City.**  
   The Loom Council officially denies their existence. Unofficially, certain wardens and guildmasters know exactly whom to pay when a problem must vanish between stitches.

## **The Sixfold Quarter**

*“One loom, six voices.”*

On the slope between The Crown and the inner Weaves lies a dignified district of plazas, gardens, and fortified houses known as the **Sixfold Quarter**. Here the six signatory peoples maintain their embassies, facing one another across shared streets rather than from walled-off enclaves.

### **The Charter of the Six**

The Quarter is governed by the Charter itself, which declares:

* No embassy is sovereign soil; city law still applies.

* No race may be confined to its embassy or any single district.

* Disputes between peoples are first heard in the Quarter before they reach the Loom Council.

A stone gate at the Quarter’s main entrance bears the inscription:

> *“One loom, six threads. No wall may divide what the Charter has bound.”*

### **Embassies of the Quarter**

**Hall of the Woven Crown (Human Seat).**  
 Anchoring the upper side of the Quarter, the Hall serves as both human embassy and council chamber. It is here that human crowns speak with a single voice—when they manage to speak together at all.

**The Verdant Spire (Elven Embassy).**  
 A garden-tower of living stone and magic-fed greenery. Elven envoys treat centuries-old agreements as “current affairs” and measure policy in lifetimes rather than terms of office.

**Stonefast Hall (Dwarven Embassy).**  
 A rune-carved, half-sunken stronghold that functions as court of record for contracts, weights, and measures. When Stonefast declares a debt valid, not even a king’s denial can easily outweigh it.

**Iron-Tusk Court (Orc Embassy).**  
 A broad courtyard ringed by clan halls and war lodges. Mercenary charters, frontier truces, and right-of-passage pacts are thrashed out here, often to the sound of practice steel in the yard.

**Ashglass Consulate (Tiefling Embassy).**  
 A dark-stone manor lit by stained-glass windows, where the tiefling diaspora finds representation. Secrets, reputations, and “off-the-record” agreements are the true currency of its halls.

**Crimson Thread House (Sangrith Embassy).**  
 A pale building stitched with crimson lines, dedicated to oaths and bargains sealed in blood and magic. Within, Sangrith oath-keepers witness pacts that even the Loom Council hesitates to overturn.

### **One Loom, Many Streets**

Though each embassy speaks for its people, their citizens are scattered throughout every Weave:

* Elves favor Skyloom’s towers and quiet gardens.  
* Dwarves cluster near forges and stoneworks in Northreach and beyond, and keep Stormwake’s bonded vaults.    
* Orcs fill barracks, caravan yards, and docks across multiple districts.  
* Tieflings appear anywhere knowledge, coin, or secrets flow.  
* Sangrith sanctums hide in back alleys and near shrines in many Weaves.  
* Humans are everywhere and claim nowhere is *truly* foreign to them.

No law draws borders between them, but old habits, shared trades, and quiet prejudice still shape where each thread most often lies.

## **Faith & Symbols**

The people of Crownweave are less united by a single god than by shared symbols.

* **Seal of the City**: a simple crown backed by three crossing threads.  
* **Common Mottos**:  
  * *“What is torn, we weave anew.”*  
  * *“Coins are thread; oaths are cloth.”*

At the edge of The Crown stands the **Temple of a Thousand Threads**, a sprawling complex where shrines to many human deities coexist. Pilgrims and priests alike treat it as sacred neutral ground—at least in theory.

## **The Sound of The Streets**

On any given day, Crownweave is:

* caravans from half the world queued at the gates,  
  hawkers calling out wares in mingled accents,  
* scribes arguing over trade clauses,  
* guards chasing a cutpurse through crowds of silk, steel, and spice.

When adventurers arrive, they’re sized up quickly: new thread, unknown pattern.

> “Welcome to Crownweave, traveler. Mind your purse, mind your tongue—and if you’re wise, mind whose thread you pull.”
